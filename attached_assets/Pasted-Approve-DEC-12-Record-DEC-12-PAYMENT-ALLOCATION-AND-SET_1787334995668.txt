DEC-12 — PAYMENT, ALLOCATION AND SETTLEMENT POLICY
STATUS: APPROVED

Approve the following accounting model:

1. DISTINCT CONCEPTS

Ledgerpoint must maintain a clear separation between:

- payment evidence;
- accounting payment;
- payment allocation;
- document settlement status.

These concepts must not be collapsed into a single object or accounting event.

2. BANK EVIDENCE

A bank-feed transaction/row is evidence.

Bank-feed evidence must NOT automatically be treated as an accounting payment merely because it exists.

Reconciliation may link bank evidence to an accounting payment.

Reconciliation must NEVER create duplicate accounting merely because evidence is linked to a payment.

3. CUSTOMER PAYMENT

A posted customer accounting payment creates one canonical journal effect:

DR Bank/Cash
CR Accounts Receivable

The payment must use the approved DEC-10 control-account mappings and applicable DEC-11 configuration version.

4. SUPPLIER PAYMENT

A posted supplier accounting payment creates one canonical journal effect:

DR Accounts Payable
CR Bank/Cash

The payment must use the approved DEC-10 control-account mappings and applicable DEC-11 configuration version.

5. ALLOCATION

Payment allocation is a first-class relationship between a payment and one or more eligible documents.

Support:

- one payment → multiple invoices/bills;
- multiple payments → one invoice/bill;
- partial payments;
- remaining/unallocated payment amounts.

Allocation must never exceed:

- the remaining payment amount; or
- the eligible document open amount,

subject to the separately approved treatment of overpayments and other later payment policies.

6. ALLOCATION DOES NOT DUPLICATE ACCOUNTING

Allocating a payment to an invoice/bill must NOT create another cash/AR/AP accounting posting merely because the allocation occurred.

The accounting payment is the canonical accounting event.

Allocation represents how that payment is applied to open items.

7. SETTLEMENT STATUS

Invoice and bill settlement status must be derived from authoritative:

- payment;
- allocation;
- open-item;
- credit-note;
- and other approved accounting data.

Settlement status must NOT be an independently editable accounting fact.

8. PARTIAL AND FULL SETTLEMENT

Support:

- unpaid;
- partially paid;
- fully settled;

as derived states.

The authoritative outstanding amount must be calculated from the canonical accounting/payment/allocation model.

9. ALLOCATION CHANGES

Changing an allocation must preserve an append-only history.

Allocation changes may use:

- reversal;
- supersession;
- replacement history;

as appropriate.

Moving a payment from one eligible document to another must NOT repost the cash or AR/AP accounting effect merely because the allocation changed.

10. HISTORICAL ACCOUNTING

Historical posted payment journals remain immutable.

Allocation changes must never:

- rewrite posted journals;
- change historical cash/bank accounting;
- silently alter historical AR/AP postings;
- erase allocation history.

Where an actual accounting correction is required, use the canonical correction/reversal mechanisms approved under DEC-04.

11. OVERPAYMENTS / UNAPPLIED CASH / PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT

DEC-12 establishes the structural prerequisites for these concepts but does NOT approve their final accounting policy or launch scope.

Their detailed treatment remains for the appropriate later decisions:

- DEC-13
- DEC-14
- DEC-15
- DEC-16

Do not infer approval of those later policies from DEC-12.

12. REFUNDS

Refund representation and policy remain subject to the appropriate later decision.

Do not implement or approve refund policy through DEC-12.

13. BANK RECONCILIATION

Maintain the separation:

BANK EVIDENCE
→ ACCOUNTING PAYMENT
→ ALLOCATION
→ SETTLEMENT

Reconciliation may establish relationships between these objects.

Reconciliation must not create duplicate canonical accounting.

14. VAT

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Payment handling must not create a second VAT engine or silently expand VAT scope.

Where payment events have VAT consequences under a future approved policy, those consequences must remain governed by DEC-03.

15. PERIOD CONTROLS

Payment postings remain subject to DEC-07.

A payment must not bypass closed-period controls.

Posting date determines the applicable accounting period.

16. CONFIGURATION

Payment posting must respect DEC-11.

Where material configuration affects payment posting:

- resolve the applicable configuration using the canonical posting date;
- record the configuration version;
- retain the resolved account IDs on the canonical posting.

17. PERMISSIONS

Payment creation, posting, allocation, reallocation and related consequential actions must use the DEC-05 server-side capability model.

Do not rely on frontend controls or arbitrary role strings.

18. AUDIT

Maintain auditable history for:

- payment creation;
- payment posting;
- allocation;
- allocation reversal;
- reallocation;
- unallocation;
- settlement-state changes.

Audit history must preserve the relevant actor, company scope, action, source and timing.

19. MIGRATION

Historical payment and allocation data must only be migrated where supported by evidence.

Never invent:

- historical payments;
- payment dates;
- allocations;
- settlement events;
- historical payment relationships.

Ambiguous historical data becomes an explicit migration exception for DEC-22.

20. SOURCE-FRESHNESS BOUNDARY

The existing source-freshness issue remains a separate unresolved posting-safety dependency.

DEC-12 does NOT approve or resolve that issue.

Do not silently reassign or absorb it into DEC-12.

21. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The approved model must support future:

- payment-on-account;
- overpayment handling;
- unapplied cash workflows;
- refunds;
- additional payment methods;
- additional currencies;
- additional markets.

Those capabilities remain subject to their own decisions.
