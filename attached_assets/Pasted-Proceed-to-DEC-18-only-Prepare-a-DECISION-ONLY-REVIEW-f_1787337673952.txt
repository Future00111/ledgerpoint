==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine Ledgerpoint's policy for what happens to information once it reaches the end of its approved retention period under DEC-17.

The review must clearly distinguish:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- archival;
- redaction;
- retention;
- legal hold;
- accounting immutability;
- personal-data minimisation.

The solution must preserve the integrity and auditability of canonical accounting history.

==================================================
1. RETENTION VS DISPOSAL
==================================================

DEC-17 determines retention.

DEC-18 determines what happens after the applicable retention requirement has expired.

Do not alter DEC-17's approved retention policy.

Define the relationship between:

RETENTION PERIOD
→ ELIGIBILITY
→ REVIEW / HOLD CHECK
→ DISPOSAL ACTION

==================================================
2. ACCOUNTING RECORDS
==================================================

Determine what can and cannot be deleted from:

- posted journals;
- source links;
- approvals;
- reversals;
- corrections;
- VAT evidence;
- reconciliation evidence;
- payment evidence;
- allocation evidence;
- customer-credit evidence;
- supplier-prepayment evidence;
- refund evidence.

Accounting history must remain consistent with DEC-04 and DEC-17.

Do not allow deletion to create unexplained accounting history.

==================================================
3. PERSONAL DATA
==================================================

Determine appropriate treatment of personal data once retention requirements expire.

Evaluate:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- minimisation;
- pseudonymisation where appropriate;
- retention where legally required.

Do not assume personal data must remain indefinitely merely because an accounting record exists.

==================================================
4. DOCUMENTS
==================================================

Determine how documents should be handled after their retention period.

Consider:

- deletion;
- archival;
- redaction;
- preservation where required;
- links from accounting records.

Do not break authoritative accounting traceability.

==================================================
5. AI DATA
==================================================

Respect DEC-17.

Determine what happens to:

- AI prompts;
- AI outputs;
- AI action evidence;
- intermediate AI data.

Retain only what is necessary to explain consequential actions for the approved period.

Do not retain unnecessary AI data indefinitely.

==================================================
6. REDACTION
==================================================

Determine whether redaction should be supported.

If yes, define:

- what may be redacted;
- what must never be redacted;
- who may authorise it;
- audit requirements;
- interaction with accounting history;
- interaction with exports and reports.

Never use redaction to alter the meaning of a posted journal.

==================================================
7. ANONYMISATION
==================================================

Determine when anonymisation is appropriate.

Consider:

- personal data;
- customer/supplier identities;
- documents;
- historical reporting.

Anonymisation must not make canonical accounting impossible to interpret where accounting records must remain legally retained.

==================================================
8. ARCHIVAL
==================================================

Determine whether archival should be a distinct lifecycle state.

If recommended, explain:

- what archival means;
- when it occurs;
- whether archived records remain queryable;
- whether archived data remains included in reports;
- whether archived records remain auditable;
- how restoration works.

Do not confuse archival with deletion.

==================================================
9. LEGAL HOLDS
==================================================

Respect DEC-17.

Determine how a legal hold prevents:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- redaction;
- destruction;

where required.

Define:

- who can place a hold;
- who can release it;
- audit;
- scope;
- precedence over normal retention expiry.

==================================================
10. COMPANY CLOSURE
==================================================

Determine how disposal works when a company is closed.

Company closure must not override required accounting retention.

Consider:

- inactive company;
- closed company;
- dissolved company;
- retention after closure;
- eventual disposal.

Do not create a new financial/accounting state unless necessary.

==================================================
11. USER RIGHTS / DATA REQUESTS
==================================================

Determine how data deletion, anonymisation or access requests interact with legally required accounting retention.

Where data must be retained:

- preserve required accounting evidence;
- minimise personal data where lawful;
- use anonymisation/redaction where legally appropriate;
- do not delete records merely because a user requests deletion if legal retention prevents it.

Keep the policy within DEC-18's registered scope.

==================================================
12. ACCOUNTING TRACEABILITY
==================================================

A disposal action must never result in:

- broken journal history;
- unexplained balances;
- altered historical reports;
- changed VAT results;
- changed AR/AP history;
- rewritten approvals;
- changed accounting meaning.

If a source document is removed, determine what minimum accounting evidence must remain.

==================================================
13. AUDIT
==================================================

Define immutable audit requirements for:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- archival;
- redaction;
- legal hold;
- legal-hold release;
- failed disposal;
- restoration where archival is supported.

Audit evidence must itself remain protected according to DEC-17.

==================================================
14. PERMISSIONS
==================================================

Use DEC-05.

Determine which actions require:

- normal capability;
- elevated capability;
- specialised authority;
- approval.

Do not rely solely on frontend controls.

==================================================
15. SECURITY / COMPANY SCOPE
==================================================

All disposal actions must remain:

- company-scoped;
- server-side;
- capability-controlled;
- auditable.

A disposal process must never cross company boundaries.

==================================================
16. REPORTING
==================================================

Determine how deletion/anonymisation/archival affects:

- Balance Sheet;
- Profit & Loss;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- customer statements;
- supplier statements;
- aged receivables;
- aged payables;
- audit views.

Historical accounting reports must not change merely because non-authoritative supporting information is disposed of.

==================================================
24. IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Data disposal must never become accounting mutation.

Deletion, anonymisation, archival and redaction are lifecycle operations.

They must never be used to:

- rewrite journals;
- alter balances;
- change historical VAT;
- conceal accounting corrections;
- remove required audit evidence.
