The purpose of this review is to establish Ledgerpoint's accounting-period policy and controls before implementation.

The review must cover:

1. PERIOD FREQUENCY

Evaluate the appropriate launch policy for:
- monthly periods
- quarterly periods
- annual-only periods
- configurable period frequency.

Recommend the most appropriate approach for Ledgerpoint while considering UK small-business accounting.

2. PERIOD IDENTITY

Define how an accounting period is uniquely identified within a company and financial year.

It must remain distinct from:
- VAT periods
- tax years
- bank statement periods
- calendar months where applicable.

3. PERIOD CREATION

Define:
- when periods are created
- whether they are automatically generated
- whether future periods can exist before the current period closes
- how periods relate to the approved DEC-06 financial-year structure.

4. OPEN / CLOSED STATES

Define the authoritative lifecycle of an accounting period.

Consider states such as:
- OPEN
- CLOSED

Do not introduce unnecessary statuses without justification.

5. POSTING INTO PERIODS

Define:
- what determines the accounting period for a posting
- whether posting date controls period assignment
- what happens when a posting date falls outside an open period
- how future-dated postings are handled
- how historical postings are handled during migration.

6. PERIOD CLOSE

Define:
- who can close a period
- required permissions under DEC-05
- whether close requires validation
- whether unresolved drafts prevent close
- whether unallocated payments prevent close
- whether unreconciled bank transactions prevent close
- whether VAT issues prevent close
- whether close produces an audit record.

Do not invent hard blockers without explaining why they are necessary.

7. PERIOD REOPEN

Define:
- who may reopen a closed period
- whether reopening requires elevated capability
- whether a reason is mandatory
- whether approval is required
- whether reopening is temporary or persistent
- what audit information is retained.

8. POSTING TO CLOSED PERIODS

Determine whether any posting can occur in a closed period.

If exceptions are recommended, define:
- who can use them
- what capability is required
- whether a reason is mandatory
- how the audit trail works
- whether the system should instead use a controlled adjustment in a later open period.

9. PERIOD LOCKING

Distinguish between:
- UI disabling
- application/server enforcement
- accounting authority.

The actual accounting restriction must be enforced server-side.

10. YEAR BOUNDARIES

Explain how DEC-07 interacts with:
- DEC-06 financial-year boundaries
- first/partial financial years
- year transitions
- DEC-08 year-end treatment.

Do NOT resolve DEC-08.

11. REPORTING

Explain how period state affects:
- Profit & Loss
- Balance Sheet
- Trial Balance
- General Ledger
- VAT reporting
- comparative reporting.

Reports must remain journal-authoritative under DEC-04.

12. MIGRATION

Explain how historical records with incomplete or ambiguous period information should be handled.

Never invent historical accounting facts.

13. AUDIT

Define the audit requirements for:
- period creation
- close
- reopen
- posting attempts into closed periods
- privileged exceptions.

14. PERMISSIONS

Map period actions to the DEC-05 capability architecture.

Do not silently grant permissions.

15. OPTIONS

Present all realistic policy options.

For each option explain:
- accounting consequences
- data/schema consequences
- migration consequences
- reporting consequences
- security/permission consequences
- operational consequences
- compatibility consequences
- future flexibility
- what becomes difficult to change later.

16. RECOMMENDATION

Provide a clearly labelled:

RECOMMENDATION — NOT APPROVAL

Do not convert the recommendation into a decision.

17. DECISION BOUNDARIES

Clearly distinguish:

ALREADY DECIDED
RECOMMENDED
REQUIRES USER DECISION
DELIBERATELY LEFT OPEN

Explicitly identify:

- what approving DEC-07 would lock in;
- what remains changeable;
- what must be decided by DEC-08;
- what decisions should remain outside DEC-07.

18. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE

Do not design the period system in a way that permits users to rewrite posted accounting history simply by changing period configuration.

The DEC-04 immutability and audit principles remain authoritative.

19. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

Do not unnecessarily prevent:
- different financial-year starts already permitted by DEC-06
- future markets
- future currencies
- future accounting/reporting requirements
- future period policies.
