DEC-22 — HISTORICAL ACCOUNTING-DATA COMPATIBILITY AND CUTOVER
STATUS: APPROVED — migration/cutover policy only

Approve the recommended validated additive-adapter model with controlled cohort/source cutover.

==================================================
1. MIGRATION PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Migration is a controlled transformation of existing evidence into Ledgerly's governed accounting model.

Migration must preserve accounting meaning wherever evidence permits.

Migration must NEVER invent:

- accounting facts;
- journal entries;
- dates;
- approvals;
- VAT treatment;
- financial-year assignments;
- accounting-period closures;
- configuration history;
- allocations;
- refunds;
- retention events;
- legal holds;
- deletion/anonymisation events.

Where evidence is incomplete:

IDENTIFY THE GAP.

Where evidence conflicts:

RECONCILE IT.

Where uncertainty remains:

RECORD THE EXCEPTION.

Never silently manufacture certainty.

==================================================
2. SOURCE EVIDENCE AND PROVENANCE
==================================================

Before transformation, preserve:

- source evidence;
- provenance;
- company ownership;
- source identifiers where appropriate;
- historical uncertainty;
- mapping decisions;
- migration exceptions.

The source must remain traceable throughout migration.

==================================================
3. SUPPORTED SOURCES AND COHORTS
==================================================

Before migration work begins, define:

- supported legacy sources;
- supported data shapes;
- mapping contracts;
- migration cohorts;
- evidence requirements;
- validation criteria.

Do not assume every legacy source or data shape is supported.

Unsupported or ambiguous source data becomes an explicit migration exception.

==================================================
4. ADDITIVE ADAPTER MODEL
==================================================

Use additive, versioned migration adapters rather than destructive replacement.

Adapters must transform supported source representations into Ledgerly's governed canonical model while preserving provenance.

Do not destructively overwrite source evidence merely to fit Ledgerly's schema.

The adapter/mapping contract must be versioned and auditable.

==================================================
5. CANONICAL ACCOUNTING AUTHORITY
==================================================

After a validated cutover:

Ledgerly's canonical posted journals become the sole reporting and accounting authority.

Do not allow two systems to silently operate as competing accounting authorities.

Legacy systems may remain as:

- read-only evidence;
- bounded comparison sources;

but they must not continue as a competing source of accounting truth after cutover.

==================================================
6. DUAL-READ COMPARISON
==================================================

Bounded dual-read comparison may be used for validation.

It is NOT a permanent dual-accounting model.

Comparison may identify:

- balance differences;
- mapping differences;
- historical interpretation differences;
- missing evidence;
- unsupported legacy features.

Dual-read comparison must never create duplicate accounting authority.

==================================================
7. MIGRATION SCOPE
==================================================

Migration scope must be explicitly defined for each cohort.

Potential migrated areas include:

- companies;
- memberships where appropriate;
- chart of accounts;
- account balances;
- journals;
- invoices;
- bills;
- credit notes;
- customers;
- suppliers;
- payments;
- allocations;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- bank accounts;
- bank/reconciliation evidence;
- VAT records;
- financial years;
- accounting periods;
- configuration;
- audit evidence;
- documents;
- retention/disposal state.

Each migration must classify data as appropriate:

- MUST MIGRATE;
- SHOULD MIGRATE;
- MAY MIGRATE;
- DO NOT MIGRATE;
- MIGRATION EXCEPTION.

Do not require migration of data that cannot be supported or evidenced safely.

==================================================
8. ACCOUNTING VALIDATION
==================================================

Before cutover, validate as appropriate:

- journal integrity;
- trial balance;
- Balance Sheet;
- Profit & Loss;
- chart-of-accounts mapping;
- control accounts;
- VAT;
- financial years;
- accounting periods;
- configuration;
- AR;
- AP;
- payments;
- allocations;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- bank balances/evidence;
- reconciliation;
- documents;
- audit history;
- retention/disposal state;
- company ownership.

No unexplained material accounting difference may be silently accepted at cutover.

==================================================
9. DEC-04 IMMUTABILITY
==================================================

Migrated canonical journals must respect DEC-04.

Migration must not be used to:

- rewrite posted history;
- fabricate corrections;
- erase discrepancies;
- silently alter historical accounting meaning.

Where a legitimate post-cutover discrepancy is discovered, use the approved canonical correction/reversal process.

==================================================
10. FINANCIAL YEARS AND PERIODS
==================================================

Respect DEC-06 and DEC-07.

Historical records must be assigned to financial years and periods using available evidence.

Do not invent:

- financial-year boundaries;
- period closures;
- reopen events;
- year-end completion events.

DEC-08 reporting-only year-end policy remains authoritative.

==================================================
11. CHART OF ACCOUNTS AND CONFIGURATION
==================================================

Respect DEC-09, DEC-10 and DEC-11.

Legacy accounts must be mapped using stable accounting identity/classification and accounting meaning.

Do not rely solely on:

- account names;
- numeric codes.

Historical control-account mappings must be preserved where evidence exists.

Unknown historical configuration becomes an explicit migration exception.

==================================================
12. VAT
==================================================

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Migration must preserve historical VAT meaning where supported by evidence.

Do not create a second VAT engine merely for migration.

Never invent historical VAT treatment.

==================================================
13. PAYMENTS AND ALLOCATIONS
==================================================

Respect DEC-12 through DEC-16.

Preserve the distinction between:

- bank evidence;
- accounting payment;
- allocation;
- settlement;
- customer credit;
- supplier prepayment;
- refund.

Do not invent historical allocations.

Where allocation evidence is incomplete, create a migration exception.

==================================================
14. DOCUMENTS AND SOURCE EVIDENCE
==================================================

Respect DEC-17 and DEC-18.

Migrate authoritative historical documents/evidence where required and supported.

Do not:

- invent documents;
- invent historical disposal events;
- recreate evidence that never existed.

Historical lifecycle state must only be migrated where supported by evidence.

==================================================
15. RETENTION AND DISPOSAL
==================================================

Respect DEC-17 and DEC-18.

Where historical evidence exists for:

- retention classifications;
- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- archival;
- redaction;
- legal holds;

preserve it appropriately.

Where evidence does not exist, do not manufacture historical lifecycle events.

Record the uncertainty as a migration exception.

==================================================
16. TENANT ISOLATION
==================================================

Respect DEC-21.

Migration must preserve company boundaries.

It must never:

- merge companies accidentally;
- assign records to the wrong company;
- create cross-company documents;
- create cross-company accounting;
- create unauthorised cross-company relationships;
- leak company-specific AI context.

Company ownership must be validated before cutover.

==================================================
17. AI IN MIGRATION
==================================================

AI may assist with:

- classification;
- mapping suggestions;
- anomaly detection;
- document interpretation;
- exception explanation.

AI must NOT:

- invent accounting facts;
- silently decide authoritative accounting;
- bypass deterministic migration rules;
- approve consequential migration decisions without required human authority.

AI-assisted migration activity must remain auditable.

==================================================
18. MIGRATION EXCEPTIONS
==================================================

Use a formal migration-exception mechanism.

Where appropriate, capture:

- company;
- source record;
- issue;
- missing evidence;
- mapping conflict;
- proposed treatment;
- decision;
- authority;
- reason;
- timestamp;
- resolution;
- residual uncertainty.

Migration exceptions must be company-scoped and auditable.

Do not hide uncertainty inside ordinary accounting records.

==================================================
19. SECURITY
==================================================

Migration is a high-risk operation.

Require appropriate:

- least privilege;
- elevated capabilities;
- approval;
- separation of duties;
- encrypted transfer;
- temporary credentials;
- audit evidence;
- source-data protection.

Migration authority must be enforced server-side.

==================================================
20. RECOVERY SAFEGUARDS
==================================================

Respect DEC-20.

Each migration/cutover cohort must have appropriate recovery safeguards, including:

- verified recovery checkpoint;
- pre-cutover snapshot/backup;
- restoration validation;
- defined recovery point;
- defined rollback criteria.

Do not proceed where required recovery safeguards have not been validated.

==================================================
21. CUTOVER
==================================================

A controlled cutover should include, as appropriate:

1. source freeze;
2. final extraction;
3. transformation;
4. validation;
5. reconciliation;
6. exception review;
7. recovery checkpoint;
8. explicit cutover approval;
9. Ledgerly activation;
10. legacy transition to read-only/comparison status.

Cutover must be an explicit controlled event.

==================================================
22. LEGACY AUTHORITY
==================================================

After cutover, the legacy system must not remain a competing accounting authority.

It may remain available as:

- read-only historical evidence;
- bounded comparison source;

until retention and audit requirements permit a later approved disposition.

Do not destroy legacy evidence prematurely.

==================================================
23. ROLLBACK
==================================================

Rollback is permitted only within clearly defined migration/cutover boundaries.

Rollback must NOT become a casual mechanism for rewriting accounting history after Ledgerly becomes authoritative.

If a material accounting discrepancy is discovered after Ledgerly has become authoritative, use DEC-04 correction/reversal mechanisms unless the controlled cutover rollback criteria are genuinely satisfied.

Rollback criteria must be explicitly defined and approved during implementation planning.

==================================================
24. POST-CUTOVER
==================================================

After cutover, provide an appropriate controlled reconciliation period including:

- enhanced monitoring;
- migration-exception resolution;
- accounting review;
- audit review;
- user/accounting confirmation;
- legacy read-only access where required.

Post-cutover discrepancies must be handled through approved accounting correction processes unless valid rollback criteria apply.

==================================================
25. NO AUTOMATIC IMPLEMENTATION AUTHORISATION
==================================================

Approval of DEC-22 does NOT authorise:

- migration execution;
- cutover execution;
- production data transformation;
- schema changes;
- migration scripts;
- adapters;
- UI;
- accounting implementation;
- infrastructure changes;
- deployment;
- publishing.

Those require a separate implementation planning and approval process.

==================================================
26. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY
==================================================

The approved policy must allow future:

- additional legacy sources;
- additional adapters;
- improved mapping;
- additional migration cohorts;
- improved validation;
- additional markets;
- additional accounting integrations.

New migration sources or materially different migration approaches must be assessed through the appropriate governance/amendment process.
