==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine Ledgerly's policy for migrating existing/historical accounting and related company data into the governed Ledgerly model and safely cutting over to the new accounting authority.

The review must establish:

- migration scope;
- migration authority;
- source-of-truth rules;
- evidence requirements;
- historical accounting treatment;
- mapping strategy;
- exception handling;
- validation;
- reconciliation;
- cutover;
- rollback;
- legacy-system authority;
- auditability;
- security;
- tenant isolation;
- retention;
- export;
- backup/recovery;
- post-cutover controls.

The migration policy must never invent accounting facts.

==================================================
1. MIGRATION PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Migration is a controlled transformation of existing evidence into Ledgerly's governed accounting model.

It is NOT permission to rewrite history.

The migration process must preserve the meaning of historical accounting wherever evidence permits.

Where evidence is insufficient:

- do not invent values;
- do not invent dates;
- do not invent journals;
- do not invent approvals;
- do not invent VAT treatment;
- do not invent configuration;
- do not invent financial-year assignments;
- do not invent period closures;
- do not invent allocations;
- do not invent refunds;
- do not invent retention events;
- do not invent legal holds;
- do not invent deletion/anonymisation events.

Represent uncertainty explicitly.

==================================================
2. SOURCE AUTHORITY
==================================================

Determine what constitutes the authoritative source during migration.

Consider:

- legacy accounting system;
- source documents;
- bank evidence;
- VAT records;
- invoices;
- bills;
- payments;
- reconciliation evidence;
- audit records;
- configuration records.

Determine precedence where multiple sources disagree.

Do not assume one source is automatically authoritative without evidence.

==================================================
3. MIGRATION SCOPE
==================================================

Determine what should be migrated.

Consider:

- companies;
- users/memberships where relevant;
- chart of accounts;
- account balances;
- journals;
- invoices;
- bills;
- credit notes;
- customers;
- suppliers;
- payments;
- allocations;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- bank accounts;
- bank/reconciliation evidence;
- VAT records;
- financial years;
- accounting periods;
- configuration;
- audit history;
- documents;
- retention/disposal state.

Clearly distinguish:

MUST MIGRATE
SHOULD MIGRATE
MAY MIGRATE
DO NOT MIGRATE
MIGRATION EXCEPTION

Do not assume everything should be migrated.

==================================================

==================================================

Respect DEC-04, DEC-06, DEC-07, DEC-08, DEC-09, DEC-10 and DEC-11.

- journal meaning;
- account identity;
- financial-year assignment;
- accounting-period assignment;
- VAT meaning;
- configuration meaning;
- AR/AP meaning.

Where historical configuration is unknown, preserve the uncertainty rather than inventing a configuration version.

- many-to-one mapping;
- unmappable accounts;
- inactive accounts;
- historical accounts;
- system/control accounts.

Mapping must preserve accounting meaning.

Do not use account names or numeric codes as the sole authority.

map into Ledgerly's protected control-account roles.

Do not silently force incompatible legacy balances into the wrong control account.

- cross-company users;
- cross-company accounting;
- cross-company AI context.

==================================================
16. AI
==================================================

Determine the appropriate role of AI in migration.

AI may potentially assist with:

- classification;
- mapping suggestions;
- anomaly detection;
- document interpretation;
- exception explanation.

AI must NOT:

- invent missing accounting facts;
- silently determine authoritative accounting;
- bypass deterministic migration rules;
- approve consequential migration decisions without the required human authority.

Any AI-assisted migration action must remain auditable.

- company counts;

Do not allow unexplained accounting differences to be silently accepted.

==================================================
18. RECONCILIATION
==================================================

Migration must include reconciliation against authoritative source evidence.

Determine how to handle:

- exact matches;
- expected presentation differences;
- rounding;
- timing differences;
- unsupported legacy features;
- missing evidence;
- genuine discrepancies.

Every unresolved material difference must become an explicit migration exception.

- decision;
- authority;
- reason;
- timestamp;
- resolution;
- residual uncertainty.

Do not hide migration uncertainty inside ordinary accounting records.

20. CUTOVER
==================================================

Determine how Ledgerly becomes the authoritative accounting system.

Consider:

- migration freeze;
- source-system freeze;
- final extraction;
- final validation;
- final reconciliation;
- approval;
- Ledgerly activation;
- legacy-system status.

Do not allow two systems to silently act as competing accounting authorities after cutover.

==================================================

==================================================

Determine what happens to the legacy system after cutover.

Possible states may include:

- read-only historical authority;
- retained evidence source;
- archived system;
- decommissioned system after approved retention.

Do not destroy legacy authority prematurely.

DEC-22 must define the controlled transition.

==================================================
22. ROLLBACK
==================================================

Determine rollback policy.

Consider:

- pre-cutover rollback;
- failed migration;
- failed validation;
- failed cutover;
- post-cutover discovery of material discrepancy.

Rollback must not be used casually to rewrite accounting history after Ledgerly becomes authoritative.

Define when rollback is possible and when controlled accounting corrections are required instead.

- audit;
- source-data protection.
