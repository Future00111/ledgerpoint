Record:

DEC-19 — EXPORT FORMATS, SCOPE, AND PERMISSIONS
STATUS: APPROVED — product/data-export policy only

Approve the bounded launch export model.

1. HUMAN-READABLE REPORT EXPORTS

Support:

- PDF;
- CSV;

for appropriate:

- financial reports;
- VAT reports;
- AR reports;
- AP reports;
- customer/supplier statements;
- ledger reports.

Exports must reflect the same canonical data and reporting rules used by Ledgerly.

2. ACCOUNTING DATA EXPORTS

Support:

- company-scoped CSV;
- versioned JSON;

for canonical posted journals and their relevant relationships.

JSON is a documented, versioned export contract.

It must NOT be a raw database dump.

3. OPERATIONAL DATA EXPORTS

Support bounded CSV exports for authorised operational information including, where applicable:

- customers;
- suppliers;
- documents;
- payments;
- allocations;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- bank information;
- reconciliation information.

The exact export must respect the user's authority and the applicable export scope.

4. AUDIT EXPORTS

Support a versioned audit export containing:

- versioned JSON;
- human-readable manifest;
- relevant accounting/audit evidence.

Documents may be included only when separately authorised and appropriate.

Audit exports must be sufficient to help explain consequential accounting activity without exposing unnecessary data.

5. DOCUMENT EXPORTS

Support:

- individual document downloads;
- bounded document bundles.

Exported documents must preserve applicable:

- retention state;
- archival state;
- redaction state;
- anonymisation state.

Exports must never recreate information that has legitimately been disposed of under DEC-18.

6. CONFIGURATION EXPORTS

Where necessary to understand historical accounting, provide versioned, interpretation-safe configuration extracts.

These must explain relevant historical accounting meaning without exposing unnecessary internal implementation details.

7. NO UNBOUNDED FULL-COMPANY ARCHIVE AT LAUNCH

Do NOT provide an unrestricted full-company database/archive export as a default launch feature.

Export scope must remain bounded, intentional and authorised.

A future broader export can be considered through the Living Product Decisions process.

8. EXPORT AUTHORITY

Exports are authorised copies or representations of Ledgerly data.

They are NOT accounting authority.

An export must never:

- create accounting;
- mutate accounting;
- modify VAT;
- modify AR/AP;
- modify settlement;
- rewrite historical journals;
- change accounting meaning.

9. CANONICAL SOURCE

All exports must derive from canonical Ledgerly data.

Respect:

- DEC-04 immutable canonical journals;
- DEC-09 stable account identity/classification;
- DEC-10 control mappings;
- DEC-11 configuration versions;
- DEC-12 payment/allocation/settlement;
- DEC-13 customer credits/supplier prepayments;
- DEC-14 unapplied-cash workflow;
- DEC-15 refunds;
- DEC-16 payment-on-account launch scope.

10. COMPANY SCOPE

Every export must be:

- company-scoped;
- server-generated;
- capability-controlled;
- auditable.

Cross-company export must never be possible through ordinary export functionality.

11. PERMISSIONS

Use DEC-05 server-side capabilities.

Differentiate appropriately between:

- normal report exports;
- accounting exports;
- operational data exports;
- audit exports;
- document exports;
- configuration exports.

Higher-risk exports may require elevated authority or approval.

Do not rely solely on frontend controls.

12. PERSONAL DATA

Exports must respect DEC-17 and DEC-18.

Include only personal data necessary for the authorised export purpose.

Where appropriate, minimise unnecessary personal information.

Do not use export functionality to bypass anonymisation, deletion or other disposal requirements.

13. DELETED / ANONYMISED DATA

Exports must respect the current authoritative state.

Do NOT recreate:

- deleted data;
- disposed data;
- anonymised identities;
- redacted information;

merely because the information existed historically.

14. RETENTION

Exports must respect DEC-17 retention policy and DEC-18 disposal policy.

Exporting data does not extend or bypass Ledgerly's retention obligations.

Exported copies are separate representations and must not be treated as a mechanism for defeating disposal requirements.

15. EXPORT AUDIT

Record appropriate immutable export evidence including:

- actor;
- company;
- export type;
- scope;
- filters;
- date/time;
- format;
- success/failure;
- access/download event where appropriate.

Export audit evidence itself remains subject to DEC-17 and DEC-18.

16. REPORT CONSISTENCY

Exported reports must use the same authoritative data and reporting logic as Ledgerly's in-product reports.

Respect:

- reporting date;
- posting date;
- financial year;
- accounting period;
- account identity;
- historical configuration.

An export must not silently reinterpret historical accounting.

17. FILTERING

Where appropriate, support user-facing filters such as:

- date range;
- financial year;
- accounting period;
- account;
- customer;
- supplier;
- document type;
- transaction type.

Filters affect the exported representation only.

They must never alter canonical accounting.

18. LARGE EXPORTS

Large exports may be generated through a controlled background process where required.

The user experience should remain simple.

Where background generation is used, support appropriate:

- progress;
- completion notification;
- expiry;
- retry;
- failure handling.

Do not expose unnecessary technical implementation details.

19. EXPORT FILE ACCESS

Export files should be protected appropriately and should not remain accessible indefinitely.

Where appropriate, use:

- finite availability;
- controlled access;
- secure delivery;
- expiry.

Exact infrastructure and backup behaviour remains outside DEC-19 where owned by DEC-20.

20. AUDIT / DOCUMENT BUNDLES

Where an audit export includes supporting documents, ensure the bundle preserves the relevant relationship between:

- canonical accounting;
- source evidence;
- approvals;
- corrections;
- reconciliation;
- payments;
- allocations;
- refunds;
- relevant configuration.

Do not include unnecessary personal data.

21. BACKUP BOUNDARY

Exports are NOT backups.

DEC-20 remains responsible for:

- backup;
- recovery;
- restoration;
- disaster recovery.

Do not use exports as a substitute for the backup architecture.

22. TENANT-ISOLATION BOUNDARY

DEC-21 remains responsible for detailed tenant-isolation architecture.

DEC-19 nevertheless requires strict company scoping for every export.

23. MIGRATION BOUNDARY

DEC-22 remains responsible for migration and cutover.

Exports may support migration preparation or historical transfer where appropriate, but DEC-19 does not approve a specific migration/cutover strategy.

Historical exports must never invent missing accounting facts.

24. VERSIONING

Machine-readable export formats, particularly JSON, must be versioned.

Changes to the export contract must preserve clear interpretation of earlier export versions.

Do not expose internal database IDs or implementation details merely because they exist.

25. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The approved policy must not unnecessarily prevent future:

- additional formats;
- accounting integrations;
- migration formats;
- external accountant workflows;
- regulatory exports;
- API-based portability;
- additional markets.

Any material expansion of export scope follows the Living Product Decisions process.
