DEC-17 — AUDIT RETENTION PERIOD
STATUS: APPROVED — product/governance policy only

Approve the recommended class-based retention policy.

1. AUTHORITATIVE ACCOUNTING EVIDENCE

Posted journals and the authoritative evidence required to explain them must be retained for:

COMPANY LIFE + THE APPLICABLE LEGAL / REGULATORY RETENTION PERIOD

This includes, where applicable:

- posted journals;
- source links;
- approvals;
- reversals;
- corrections;
- VAT evidence;
- reconciliation evidence;
- payment evidence;
- allocation evidence;
- customer-credit evidence;
- supplier-prepayment evidence;
- refund evidence.

2. ACCOUNTING TRACEABILITY

Evidence must remain sufficient to explain and reproduce the history of consequential accounting activity.

Do not retain a journal without the evidence required to understand why it exists where that evidence is part of the authoritative accounting record.

3. CLASS-BASED RETENTION

Do NOT impose one universal retention period on every Ledgerly record.

Use proportionate retention classes for:

- authoritative accounting evidence;
- personal data;
- accounting/business documents;
- AI actions and outputs.

Each class must have an explicit retention rationale.

4. AI EVIDENCE

AI is not the accounting authority.

Retain only the AI evidence necessary to explain consequential accounting actions for as long as the corresponding accounting evidence requires it.

Do not retain unnecessary AI conversations, prompts, outputs or intermediate information indefinitely merely because they existed.

Where AI materially contributed to a consequential action, retain sufficient evidence to explain that contribution.

5. PERSONAL DATA

Personal data must have its own retention classification.

Do not automatically retain personal data indefinitely merely because an accounting record exists.

Where legally permissible and appropriate, personal-data retention should be minimised while preserving the accounting evidence that must legally remain.

DEC-18 remains responsible for the detailed deletion, anonymisation, archival and redaction mechanisms.

6. DOCUMENTS

Accounting documents must have an appropriate retention class based on their accounting/legal significance.

Do not assume every uploaded document requires identical retention.

Documents that form part of authoritative accounting evidence must remain available for the applicable required period.

7. RETENTION TRIGGERS

Retention must use explicit, documented triggers.

Consider:

- company creation;
- accounting-record creation/posting;
- company closure;
- relevant legal/regulatory event;
- end of applicable statutory period.

Do not rely on ambiguous timestamps or informal assumptions.

8. COMPANY CLOSURE

Company closure must not automatically make required accounting evidence eligible for deletion.

Required accounting and audit evidence continues to be retained for the applicable legal period after closure.

The exact closure/retention interaction must be explicit and auditable.

9. LEGAL HOLDS

Support an explicit legal-hold concept.

Where a valid legal hold applies, eligible records must not be destroyed merely because their normal retention period has expired.

Legal holds must be auditable.

10. RETENTION-POLICY CHANGES

Changes to retention policy must be:

- controlled;
- authorised;
- company-scoped where applicable;
- server-side enforced;
- audited.

A later policy change must not silently rewrite historical retention decisions or destroy records that must still be retained.

11. COMPANY SCOPE

Retained records must remain company-scoped.

Retention processing must never expose or mix records between companies.

12. CAPABILITY CONTROL

Consequential retention-policy actions must use the approved DEC-05 server-side capability model.

Do not rely solely on frontend permissions or arbitrary role strings.

13. IMMUTABLE AUDIT

Retention-related audit evidence must itself remain protected against unauthorised alteration.

Where an action affects the retention state of a record, preserve evidence of:

- actor;
- company;
- action;
- reason where required;
- timestamp;
- affected record/class;
- applicable policy;
- resulting state.

14. ACCOUNTING IMMUTABILITY

Retention must NEVER be used as a mechanism for rewriting accounting history.

Required posted journals remain governed by DEC-04.

Retention policy determines how long evidence remains available.

It does not change the accounting meaning of historical records.

15. DEC-18 BOUNDARY

DEC-17 does NOT decide the detailed mechanics of:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- archival;
- redaction;
- destruction;
- subject-access-related data handling.

Those remain DEC-18 responsibilities.

DEC-18 must build on the retention classes and periods approved here.

16. DEC-19 BOUNDARY

DEC-17 does not determine the detailed export policy.

DEC-19 remains responsible for export scope, formats, permissions and related behaviour.

17. DEC-20 BOUNDARY

DEC-17 does not determine backup and recovery policy.

DEC-20 remains responsible for:

- backup;
- recovery;
- restoration;
- retention of backups;
- disaster recovery.

18. DEC-21 BOUNDARY

DEC-17 does not determine tenant-isolation architecture.

DEC-21 remains responsible for the detailed isolation policy.

19. DEC-22 BOUNDARY

Historical retention state and evidence during migration must only be established from available evidence.

Never invent:

- historical retention periods;
- historical legal holds;
- historical deletion events;
- historical archival events;
- historical retention classifications.

Any unresolved historical retention evidence becomes a DEC-22 migration exception.

20. SOURCE FRESHNESS

The separately identified source-freshness/posting-safety dependency remains unresolved.

DEC-17 does not resolve or absorb it.

21. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The policy must allow future changes to individual retention classes where justified by:

- legislation;
- regulation;
- product scope;
- market requirements;
- data-protection requirements.

Changes must follow the Living Product Decisions process and must not compromise existing accounting-history obligations.
