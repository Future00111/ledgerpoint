==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine the appropriate Chart of Accounts policy for Ledgerpoint.

The goal is to establish a useful UK small-business default chart while ensuring that the canonical accounting engine remains safe, auditable and configurable.

==================================================
1. DEFAULT CHART
==================================================

Evaluate the appropriate default Chart of Accounts for a UK-focused small-business accounting product.

Determine:

- account numbering approach;
- account names;
- account types;
- account categories;
- reporting classifications;
- parent/child hierarchy where appropriate.

The chart should be practical rather than unnecessarily large.

Avoid copying a traditional accounting chart simply because it is familiar.

The chart must support the approved Ledgerpoint product scope.

==================================================
2. REQUIRED ACCOUNT TYPES
==================================================

Evaluate the required account classifications, including:

- Assets
- Liabilities
- Equity
- Revenue/Income
- Cost of Sales
- Expenses

Also determine the appropriate treatment/category for:

- Bank/Cash
- Accounts Receivable
- Accounts Payable
- VAT/tax
- retained results/equity presentation
- other required control accounts.

==================================================
3. ACCOUNT CODES
==================================================

Recommend a numbering convention.

Consider:

- readability;
- reporting;
- expansion;
- custom accounts;
- future markets;
- future currencies;
- migration compatibility.

Do not unnecessarily hard-code account numbers if doing so would prevent future evolution.

==================================================
4. CONTROL ACCOUNTS
==================================================

Identify accounts that must be treated as controlled/system accounts.

At minimum assess:

- Accounts Receivable
- Accounts Payable
- Bank/Cash
- VAT Output
- VAT Input
- VAT Control/Settlement where appropriate
- Revenue
- Cost of Sales
- Expenses
- Equity/retained results where relevant.

Explain which accounts:

- users may edit;
- users may rename;
- users may deactivate;
- users must not delete;
- users must not manually post to;
- require elevated capability.

Do NOT resolve DEC-10's full control-account mapping policy.

Clearly identify what belongs in DEC-10.

==================================================
5. USER CUSTOMISATION
==================================================

Determine what companies should be able to customise.

Consider:

- create account;
- rename account;
- change reporting category;
- change account code;
- deactivate account;
- archive account;
- merge accounts;
- delete accounts.

Define safe restrictions.

The accounting engine must never allow customisation that breaks:

- double-entry;
- VAT;
- AR;
- AP;
- bank accounting;
- reporting;
- period/year-end integrity.

==================================================
6. SYSTEM / DEFAULT ACCOUNTS

Define how Ledgerpoint distinguishes:

- system accounts;
- default accounts;
- user-created accounts;
- inactive accounts;
- historical accounts.

Determine whether system accounts can be overridden and under what conditions.

==================================================
7. REPORTING CLASSIFICATION

Ensure every account can map appropriately into:

- Profit & Loss;
- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- VAT reporting where applicable.

The reporting classification must not depend on fragile frontend assumptions.

==================================================
8. INVOICE / BILL / PAYMENT MAPPINGS

Review how the default chart should support mappings for:

SALES INVOICES:
- AR
- revenue
- output VAT

SUPPLIER BILLS:
- AP
- expense/asset
- input VAT

CUSTOMER PAYMENTS:
- bank/cash
- AR

SUPPLIER PAYMENTS:
- AP
- bank/cash

CREDIT NOTES:
- corresponding reversal/reduction accounts

Do not implement these mappings.

Identify dependencies with DEC-10.

==================================================
9. VAT

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Determine how VAT-related accounts should exist within the chart without creating a second VAT engine.

Do not expand VAT scope beyond DEC-03.

==================================================
10. YEAR-END

Respect DEC-08.

Do not create automatic year-end closing or retained-earnings journals.

Determine how equity/retained-results accounts should be represented for reporting without conflicting with the approved reporting-only year-end policy.

==================================================
11. ACCOUNT LIFECYCLE

Define recommended lifecycle states/behaviour for accounts.

Consider:

- active;
- inactive;
- historical;
- system.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

Determine what happens if an account is no longer used.

Never allow historical accounting records to lose their account reference simply because an account becomes inactive.

==================================================
12. ACCOUNT DELETION

Determine whether accounts can ever be physically deleted.

Pay particular attention to accounts referenced by:

- posted journals;
- VAT records;
- invoices;
- bills;
- payments;
- reports.

Do not allow deletion to corrupt historical accounting.

==================================================
13. ACCOUNT MERGING

Determine whether account merging should be supported.

If recommended, explain how it can be performed without rewriting historical journals.

If not recommended for launch, explicitly identify it as future scope.

==================================================
14. MULTI-CURRENCY / FUTURE MARKETS

DEC-02 established UK/GBP launch direction but deliberately avoided permanent hard-coding.

The chart architecture should not unnecessarily prevent future:

- currencies;
- markets;
- tax systems;
- reporting requirements.

Do not implement internationalisation.

==================================================
15. PERMISSIONS

Map account-management actions to DEC-05.

Determine which actions should require elevated capabilities.

Do not silently grant permissions.

==================================================
16. MIGRATION

Review how the existing Ledgerpoint chart/accounts should map into the proposed model.

Historical migration must be evidence-based.

Never invent account mappings where evidence does not exist.

Identify ambiguous mappings as migration exceptions.

==================================================
17. OPTIONS

Present realistic options for:

- default chart size;
- numbering system;
- system-account treatment;
- user customisation;
- account deletion;
- account merging;
- reporting classification.

For each material option explain:

- accounting consequences;
- data/schema consequences;
- migration consequences;
- reporting consequences;
- security/permission consequences;
- compatibility consequences;
- future flexibility;
- what becomes difficult to change later.

==================================================

- simple user experience;
- strong accounting integrity;
- DEC-04 canonical accounting;
- DEC-05 capability controls;
- DEC-08 reporting-only year-end;
- future extensibility.

==================================================
20. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE
==================================================

The Chart of Accounts is part of the accounting foundation.

However, the chart must NOT become an uncontrolled mechanism for changing accounting logic.

A user changing the name or code of an account must not change the accounting meaning of historical posted journals.

Do not use account names as accounting logic.

Use stable identifiers and explicit account classifications/mappings.

==================================================
21. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY
==================================================

The architecture must allow the Chart of Accounts to evolve through controlled amendments.

Do not permanently lock:

- account names;
- account codes;
- optional reporting categories;
- future account types

unless there is a genuine accounting reason.

System/control-account safety boundaries must remain protected.
