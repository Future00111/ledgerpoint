==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine Ledgerly's export policy.

The review must establish what users can export, who can export it, how exports are generated, what formats are supported, what accounting/audit guarantees exports provide, and how export interacts with:

- canonical accounting;
- company scope;
- permissions;
- retention;
- deletion/anonymisation;
- backups;
- migration;
- privacy;
- auditability.

Exports must never alter canonical accounting data.

==================================================
1. EXACT EXPORT SCOPE
==================================================

Determine what Ledgerly should support exporting at launch.

Consider:

- accounting records;
- journals;
- general ledger;
- trial balance;
- Balance Sheet;
- Profit & Loss;
- VAT information;
- AR;
- AP;
- customers;
- suppliers;
- invoices;
- bills;
- payments;
- allocations;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- bank/reconciliation information;
- audit evidence;
- source documents;
- configuration;
- company information.

Clearly distinguish:

- accounting exports;
- operational exports;
- audit exports;
- document exports;
- configuration exports.

Do not assume every category must be exportable at launch.

==================================================
2. EXPORT PURPOSES
==================================================

Consider legitimate export purposes such as:

- accountant review;
- statutory/accounting records;
- management reporting;
- data portability;
- audit;
- migration;
- external analysis;
- business closure;
- customer support.

Determine which purposes should be explicitly supported.

==================================================
3. EXPORT FORMATS
==================================================

Evaluate appropriate formats, including where relevant:

- CSV;
- XLSX;
- PDF;
- JSON;
- structured accounting formats;
- ZIP/document bundles.

Consider which formats are appropriate for:

- human-readable reports;
- machine-readable accounting data;
- audit evidence;
- documents.

Do not expose internal database structures merely because JSON is technically convenient.

==================================================
4. CANONICAL ACCOUNTING
==================================================

Exports must derive from canonical accounting data.

Respect:

- DEC-04 immutable journals;
- DEC-09 stable account identity/classification;
- DEC-10 control mappings;
- DEC-11 configuration versions;
- DEC-12 payment/allocation/settlement;
- DEC-13 customer credits/supplier prepayments;
- DEC-14 unapplied-cash workflow;
- DEC-15 refunds;
- DEC-16 payment-on-account launch scope.

An export must never:

- create accounting;
- modify accounting;
- reinterpret historical journals;
- silently recalculate historical accounting;
- change VAT;
- change AR/AP;
- change settlement.

==================================================
5. REPORTING CONSISTENCY
==================================================

Determine how exported reports remain consistent with in-product reports.

Consider:

- reporting date;
- posting date;
- financial year;
- accounting period;
- closed periods;
- configuration version;
- historical account identity.

Exports should reflect the same authoritative data used by Ledgerly's reports.

==================================================
6. AUDIT EXPORTS
==================================================

Determine whether Ledgerly should support an audit-oriented export containing enough evidence to explain consequential accounting activity.

Consider:

- journals;
- source links;
- approvals;
- corrections;
- VAT evidence;
- reconciliation evidence;
- payment/allocation history;
- refunds;
- relevant configuration versions;
- audit trail.

Do not export unnecessary personal data.

==================================================
7. SOURCE DOCUMENTS
==================================================

Determine whether users can export/download:

- invoices;
- bills;
- receipts;
- credit notes;
- supporting documents;
- other uploaded accounting evidence.

Consider whether documents should be:

- individual downloads;
- bulk bundles;
- included in audit exports.

Respect DEC-17 and DEC-18.

==================================================
8. PERSONAL DATA
==================================================

Respect DEC-17 and DEC-18.

Determine:

- whether exports include personal data;
- whether export scope can be minimised;
- whether sensitive data should require elevated authority;
- whether exports need warnings or confirmation;
- whether export activity itself should be audited.

Do not create a separate privacy policy through DEC-19.

==================================================
9. PERMISSIONS
==================================================

Use DEC-05.

Determine capabilities for:

- standard reporting exports;
- accounting exports;
- customer/supplier data exports;
- audit exports;
- document exports;
- full company exports.

Consider elevated authority for high-risk exports.

Do not rely solely on frontend controls.

==================================================
10. COMPANY SCOPE / SECURITY
==================================================

All exports must be:

- company-scoped;
- server-side generated;
- capability-controlled;
- auditable.

Prevent cross-company data leakage.

Consider whether export files should have:

- expiry;
- access controls;
- download restrictions;
- secure delivery.

==================================================
11. EXPORT HISTORY / AUDIT

Determine what should be recorded when an export occurs.

Consider:

- actor;
- company;
- export type;
- scope;
- filters;
- date/time;
- format;
- success/failure;
- download/access event where appropriate.

Export audit records must be protected under DEC-17/18.

==================================================
12. LARGE EXPORTS
==================================================

Determine how large exports should work.

Consider:

- synchronous export;
- background generation;
- progress;
- completion notification;
- expiry;
- retry;
- failure handling.

Avoid exposing technical complexity to normal users.

==================================================
13. FILTERING
==================================================

Determine appropriate user-facing filters such as:

- date range;
- financial year;
- accounting period;
- account;
- customer;
- supplier;
- document type;
- transaction type.

Filters must never cause historical accounting to be reinterpreted.

==================================================
14. CONFIGURATION / HISTORY
==================================================

Where an export includes accounting configuration, determine how to represent:

- historical account identity;
- configuration version;
- effective date;
- resolved account IDs where appropriate.

Do not expose raw internal IDs to ordinary users unless there is a legitimate technical export requirement.

==================================================
15. DELETED / ANONYMISED DATA
==================================================

Respect DEC-18.

Determine how exports behave when:

- data has been deleted;
- personal data has been anonymised;
- documents have been archived;
- information is under legal hold.

Do not recreate disposed data merely because it once existed.

==================================================
16. BACKUPS
==================================================

DEC-20 owns backup and recovery.

DEC-19 should not define backup architecture.

Only determine whether exports are a user-facing data-portability/reporting mechanism distinct from backups.

==================================================
17. TENANT ISOLATION
==================================================

DEC-21 owns detailed tenant isolation.

DEC-19 must nevertheless require strict company scoping for every export.

==================================================
18. MIGRATION
==================================================

DEC-22 owns migration/cutover.

Determine whether export should support:

- migration preparation;
- legacy system transition;
- full historical export.

Do not resolve DEC-22.

Historical exports must reflect available evidence and must not invent missing accounting facts.

==================================================
22. IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Export is a copy/representation of authorised data.

Export must NEVER:

- mutate accounting;
- create accounting;
- rewrite history;
- bypass permissions;
- bypass retention;
- restore deleted data;
- cross company boundaries.

Ledgerly should make exporting information easy without making the user understand the underlying technical architecture.
