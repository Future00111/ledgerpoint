DEC-16 — PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT LAUNCH SCOPE
STATUS: APPROVED — product/accounting launch-scope policy only

Approve the recommendation that Ledgerly will NOT expose a separate user-facing Payment-on-Account feature at launch.

1. LAUNCH SCOPE

Do not create a separate Payment-on-Account feature, workflow, navigation item, accounting object, or journal type at launch.

Users should instead use the existing approved foundation:

- known-party accounting payments;
- DEC-14 Unapplied Cash workspace;
- DEC-13 customer-credit liabilities;
- DEC-13 supplier-prepayment assets;
- explicit allocation;
- append-only reallocation history;
- DEC-15 controlled refunds.

2. PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

Do not introduce an additional user-facing state merely because the underlying accounting concept exists.

The launch experience should remain:

- simple;
- clear;
- understandable;
- accounting-correct;
- consistent.

Avoid unnecessary product terminology and duplicate workflows.

3. CUSTOMER PAYMENTS

A known-party customer payment may exist before an invoice is identified.

Where the amount remains unapplied, use the approved DEC-13 customer-credit representation and DEC-14 workflow.

Do not label this as a separate Payment-on-Account product feature at launch.

4. SUPPLIER PAYMENTS

A known-party supplier payment may exist before a bill is identified.

Where the amount remains unapplied, use the approved DEC-13 supplier-prepayment representation and DEC-14 workflow.

Do not label this as a separate Payment-on-Account product feature at launch.

5. NO DUPLICATE ACCOUNTING

Do not create:

- a separate payment-on-account journal type;
- duplicate cash accounting;
- duplicate allocation accounting;
- duplicate settlement accounting;
- duplicate refund accounting.

The existing DEC-12 and DEC-13 accounting model remains authoritative.

6. ALLOCATION

Allocation continues to follow DEC-12 and DEC-14.

Users may later allocate eligible customer credits or supplier prepayments to appropriate invoices/bills.

Allocation must not repost the original cash/payment accounting merely because the allocation occurs.

7. REFUNDS

Refunds continue to follow DEC-15.

A customer-credit liability or supplier-prepayment asset may become refundable where the approved DEC-15 conditions are satisfied.

Do not create a separate Payment-on-Account refund mechanism.

8. USER EXPERIENCE

Do not expose accounting implementation details to ordinary users.

Use clear business language explaining:

- money received/paid;
- amount not yet allocated;
- customer credit;
- supplier prepayment;
- available actions.

Do not require users to understand accounting terminology simply to resolve an unapplied balance.

9. REPORTING

Customer credits remain separate from aged receivables.

Supplier prepayments remain separate from aged payables.

Do not introduce a separate Payment-on-Account reporting category at launch unless required by an already approved accounting/reporting policy.

10. VAT

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

The absence of a user-facing Payment-on-Account feature must not create a new VAT mechanism.

Do not infer VAT treatment merely from user-facing labels.

11. BANK RECONCILIATION

Maintain the approved distinction between:

BANK EVIDENCE
→ ACCOUNTING PAYMENT
→ UNAPPLIED/CREDIT/PREPAYMENT
→ ALLOCATION
→ REFUND

Reconciliation must not create duplicate accounting.

12. PERMISSIONS

Use DEC-05 for all consequential actions.

Do not create separate Payment-on-Account permissions at launch because the feature itself is not exposed.

13. AUDIT

All underlying payment, credit, prepayment, allocation and refund activity continues to use the audit requirements already established by DEC-12 through DEC-15.

Do not create a separate audit model for Payment-on-Account.

14. FUTURE EXTENSIBILITY

This decision does NOT permanently prohibit Payment-on-Account.

The architecture must remain capable of supporting a future deliberate Payment-on-Account classification using the existing accounting model.

If later introduced:

- it should initially be a user-facing classification/workflow;
- it should NOT automatically become a separate accounting object;
- it should NOT create a separate journal model;
- customer and supplier treatment should be evaluated symmetrically;
- it requires a new approved product decision before implementation.

15. MIGRATION

Do not invent historical Payment-on-Account states.

Historical advance payments, deposits, credits or prepayments must be migrated according to the evidence available and the applicable approved policies.

Ambiguity remains subject to DEC-22.

16. SOURCE FRESHNESS

The separately identified source-freshness/posting-safety dependency remains unresolved.

DEC-16 does not resolve or absorb it.
