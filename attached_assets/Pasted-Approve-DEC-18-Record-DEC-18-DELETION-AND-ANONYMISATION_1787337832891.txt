DEC-18 — DELETION AND ANONYMISATION POLICY
STATUS: APPROVED — product/governance/data-lifecycle policy only

Approve the controlled, class-based disposal model with protected accounting.

1. DISPOSAL LIFECYCLE

Use the following lifecycle:

RETENTION PERIOD
→ ELIGIBILITY
→ REVIEW / LEGAL-HOLD CHECK
→ AUTHORISED DISPOSAL ACTION

Retention expiry creates eligibility only.

It must NOT automatically trigger deletion.

2. ACCOUNTING PROTECTION

Never delete, anonymise, archive or redact a posted journal or required accounting/audit evidence in a way that:

- changes its accounting meaning;
- breaks reconstruction;
- creates unexplained balances;
- changes historical reporting;
- changes VAT results;
- alters AR/AP history;
- removes required audit evidence.

DEC-04 canonical accounting and DEC-17 retention remain authoritative.

3. ELIGIBLE DELETION

Controlled deletion may be permitted for eligible:

- drafts;
- duplicates;
- temporary artefacts;
- non-authoritative data.

Only where there is no:

- accounting dependency;
- audit dependency;
- legal hold;
- migration dependency;
- recovery dependency;
- reporting dependency;
- other required retention obligation.

4. PERSONAL DATA

Personal data should be minimised where lawful and appropriate.

Where the retention requirement for the personal data has expired, use the appropriate mechanism:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- pseudonymisation;
- redaction;

provided the retained accounting evidence remains intelligible and legally sufficient.

Do not retain personal data indefinitely merely because an accounting record remains.

5. AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE DOCUMENTS

Authoritative accounting source documents remain protected evidence for the applicable DEC-17 retention period.

Where appropriate, archival or redacted derivatives may exist, but they must not silently replace required authoritative evidence.

Do not dispose of an authoritative source merely because a derivative exists.

6. AI DATA

Retain only the AI evidence required to explain consequential accounting actions for the applicable retention period.

Unnecessary:

- prompts;
- intermediate material;
- temporary AI outputs;

may expire according to their appropriate retention class.

Do not retain unnecessary AI data indefinitely.

7. ARCHIVAL

Treat archival as a distinct lifecycle state.

Archival is NOT deletion.

Archived information must remain:

- identifiable;
- appropriately protected;
- auditable;
- available where required.

Archival must not change the meaning of canonical accounting records.

8. REDACTION

Redaction may be used where legally or operationally appropriate.

Redaction must never alter the meaning of a posted journal or remove evidence required to reconstruct consequential accounting.

Any consequential redaction must be controlled and auditable.

9. ANONYMISATION

Anonymisation may be used where lawful and appropriate, particularly for personal data.

Anonymisation must not make required canonical accounting unintelligible.

Where an accounting record must remain identifiable for legal/accounting purposes, preserve the minimum necessary identity/evidence required.

10. LEGAL HOLDS

Legal holds override normal disposal eligibility where required.

A valid hold must prevent:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- redaction;
- destruction;
- archival actions that would remove required access.

Legal holds and releases must be auditable.

11. COMPANY CLOSURE

Company closure does not override DEC-17 retention requirements.

Required accounting evidence remains protected after company closure for the applicable legal/regulatory period.

Only after the relevant retention and hold requirements have been satisfied may eligible information proceed through the disposal lifecycle.

12. DATA REQUESTS

A data request must not automatically override legal/accounting retention.

Where information must legally remain:

- preserve required accounting evidence;
- minimise personal data where lawful;
- anonymise/redact where appropriate;
- retain only what is necessary.

Do not delete required accounting evidence simply because deletion has been requested.

13. ACCOUNTING TRACEABILITY

Disposal must never result in:

- broken journal history;
- unexplained balances;
- changed historical reports;
- changed VAT results;
- rewritten approvals;
- altered accounting meaning.

If supporting information is disposed of, the remaining accounting record must retain sufficient authoritative evidence for the applicable requirement.

14. AUDIT

Maintain immutable audit evidence for consequential disposal activity, including where applicable:

- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- archival;
- redaction;
- legal-hold creation;
- legal-hold release;
- failed disposal;
- restoration of archived information.

Audit evidence itself remains subject to DEC-17.

15. PERMISSIONS

Use DEC-05 server-side capabilities.

Consequential disposal actions must not rely solely on frontend controls.

Where appropriate, use elevated or specialised authority and approval.

16. COMPANY SCOPE

All disposal operations must remain:

- company-scoped;
- server-side;
- capability-controlled;
- auditable.

A disposal operation must never cross company boundaries.

17. REPORTING

Disposal of non-authoritative supporting information must not alter canonical accounting reports.

Balance Sheet, Profit & Loss, Trial Balance, General Ledger, customer statements, supplier statements, AR/AP and audit views must continue to derive from authoritative accounting data.

18. EXPORT BOUNDARY

DEC-19 remains responsible for export policy.

DEC-18 does not determine export formats, scope, permissions or detailed export behaviour.

19. BACKUP BOUNDARY

DEC-20 remains responsible for backup and recovery.

DEC-18 does not determine the complete backup architecture or recovery policy.

20. TENANT-ISOLATION BOUNDARY

DEC-21 remains responsible for detailed tenant-isolation policy.

DEC-18 nevertheless requires disposal operations to remain company-scoped.

21. MIGRATION

Historical deletion, anonymisation, archival, redaction and legal-hold states must only be migrated where supported by evidence.

Never invent historical lifecycle events.

Ambiguity becomes an explicit DEC-22 migration exception.

22. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The approved policy should allow future changes to:

- privacy requirements;
- legal requirements;
- regulatory retention classes;
- anonymisation methods;
- archival mechanisms;
- market-specific requirements.

Any material policy change must follow the Living Product Decisions process.
