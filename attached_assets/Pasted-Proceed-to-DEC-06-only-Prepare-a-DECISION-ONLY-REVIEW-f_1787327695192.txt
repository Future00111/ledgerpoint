The purpose of this review is to establish exactly what financial-year policy Ledgerly should use before the accounting-core implementation begins.

The review must cover:

1. Financial-year start date
   - What should the default UK small-business financial year be?
   - Should companies be able to choose a different year start?
   - What happens when a company changes its financial year?

2. Financial-year identity
   Define how a financial year is identified independently from ordinary transaction/document dates.

3. Company configuration
   Determine which financial-year settings belong to the company and which are system defaults.

4. Historical years
   Define how historical financial years are represented.
   Do not invent historical accounting data.

5. Existing data compatibility
   Review how the current Ledgerpoint data and legacy records would map to financial years.

6. Changes after transactions exist
   Determine what happens if a company attempts to change its financial-year start after accounting records already exist.

7. Boundary conditions
   Consider:
   - first financial year
   - partial first year
   - year transitions
   - leap years
   - transactions on boundary dates
   - historical migration.

8. Interaction with accounting periods
   Explain what DEC-06 must establish for DEC-07 without resolving DEC-07.

9. Interaction with year-end
   Explain what DEC-06 establishes for DEC-08 without resolving DEC-08.

10. Reporting
    Explain how financial-year identity affects:
    - Profit & Loss
    - Balance Sheet
    - Trial Balance
    - General Ledger
    - VAT reporting where relevant
    - comparative reporting.

11. Audit and permissions
    Identify which financial-year changes should be privileged, auditable, and subject to the DEC-05 capability model.

12. Future flexibility
    Ensure the decision does not unnecessarily prevent:
    - different financial-year starts
    - future international markets
    - future multi-currency support
    - future changes in product policy.

13. Options
    Present all realistic policy options.

14. Recommendation
    Give a clearly labelled RECOMMENDATION.

Do NOT treat the recommendation as approval.

For each material option explain:

- accounting consequences
- data/schema consequences
- migration consequences
- reporting consequences
- security/permission consequences
- compatibility consequences
- future flexibility
- what would become difficult to change later.

DELIBERATELY LEFT OPEN
