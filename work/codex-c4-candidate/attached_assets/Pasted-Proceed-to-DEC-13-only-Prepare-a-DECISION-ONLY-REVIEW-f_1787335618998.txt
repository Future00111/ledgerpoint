==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine Ledgerly's policy for:

- customer overpayments;
- supplier overpayments;
- unapplied customer cash;
- unapplied supplier cash;
- payment-on-account;
- allocation of previously unapplied or on-account amounts;
- treatment of excess amounts;
- subsequent refunds where applicable.

The review must build directly on DEC-12.

Clearly distinguish:

1. BANK EVIDENCE
2. ACCOUNTING PAYMENT
3. UNAPPLIED PAYMENT
4. PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT
5. OVERPAYMENT
6. ALLOCATION
7. REFUND
8. DOCUMENT SETTLEMENT

==================================================
1. CUSTOMER OVERPAYMENTS
==================================================

Determine the accounting and product treatment when a customer pays more than the amount currently due.

Consider:

- payment greater than one invoice;
- payment greater than multiple invoices;
- payment exceeding all currently outstanding customer invoices;
- excess remaining after allocation.

Evaluate whether the excess should become:

- unapplied customer cash;
- customer credit/on-account balance;
- a separate liability;
- another controlled representation.

Explain the accounting consequences.

==================================================
2. SUPPLIER OVERPAYMENTS
==================================================

Determine the treatment when a company pays a supplier more than the amount currently due.

Consider whether the excess should become:

- unapplied supplier payment;
- supplier debit/on-account balance;
- an asset/receivable;
- another controlled representation.

Explain the accounting consequences.

==================================================
3. UNAPPLIED CASH
==================================================

Define what an unapplied payment means.

Determine whether a payment can exist as:

- posted accounting payment;
- unallocated remainder;
- unapplied balance;

without being allocated to a specific invoice or bill.

Explain how this differs from:

- bank evidence awaiting reconciliation;
- an accounting payment awaiting allocation;
- payment-on-account.

==================================================
4. PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT
==================================================

Determine whether Ledgerly should support payment-on-account at launch.

If yes, define:

- accounting representation;
- customer/supplier balance;
- relationship to AR/AP;
- allocation later;
- reporting;
- audit;
- refund implications.

If no, explain how users should handle the equivalent situation without creating misleading accounting.

==================================================
5. ACCOUNTING TREATMENT
==================================================

For each supported state, define the canonical accounting treatment.

Consider examples such as:

CUSTOMER PAYMENT

DR Bank/Cash
CR AR

Then determine what happens to any amount that remains unapplied/overpaid.

SUPPLIER PAYMENT

DR AP
CR Bank/Cash

Then determine what happens to any amount that remains unapplied/overpaid.

Do not create accounting entries merely to represent UI states.

==================================================
6. BALANCE SHEET TREATMENT
==================================================

Explain where customer and supplier overpayments appear on the Balance Sheet.

Pay particular attention to the distinction between:

- customer overpayment potentially creating a liability;
- supplier overpayment potentially creating a receivable/asset;
- normal AR/AP balances.

Do not allow an overpayment to make AR/AP reporting misleading.

==================================================
7. VAT
==================================================

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Determine whether overpayments or payment-on-account affect VAT treatment under the approved VAT policy.

Do not invent new VAT rules.

Do not create a second VAT engine.

Clearly identify any VAT policy questions that must remain outside DEC-13.

==================================================
8. ALLOCATION LATER

Determine how an unapplied/on-account amount can later be allocated.

Examples:

- customer pays £1,000;
- £700 allocated to Invoice A;
- £300 remains unapplied;
- later Invoice B is raised;
- £300 is allocated to Invoice B.

Determine whether allocation creates additional accounting or merely changes settlement/open-item relationships.

Respect DEC-12.

==================================================
9. REALLOCATION

Determine how an unapplied/on-account amount can be reallocated.

Examples:

- payment allocated to Invoice A;
- allocation removed;
- amount becomes unapplied;
- amount then allocated to Invoice B.

Preserve append-only allocation history.

Do not silently rewrite the original payment journal.

==================================================
10. REFUNDS

Determine the dependency between overpayments and refunds.

Do NOT fully resolve the refund policy if that belongs to DEC-14/15/16.

Instead define:

- what DEC-13 must establish;
- what the later refund decision must establish;
- how the two decisions must interact.

==================================================
11. CUSTOMER / SUPPLIER BALANCES

Define how unapplied/on-account amounts affect:

- customer balance;
- supplier balance;
- aged receivables;
- aged payables;
- statements;
- settlement reporting.

Avoid presenting an unapplied customer payment as if it were an unpaid customer invoice.

==================================================

- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- customer statements;
- supplier statements;
- aged receivables;
- aged payables;
- cash/bank reporting.

Reporting must remain derived from canonical accounting data.

==================================================
13. PERIOD CONTROLS

Respect DEC-07.

Determine how:

- payment date;
- allocation date;
- reallocation date;
- refund date

interact with open and closed accounting periods.

Do not permit these actions to bypass period controls.

==================================================
14. CONFIGURATION

Respect DEC-10 and DEC-11.

- identify the required mapping;
- resolve it using the applicable configuration version;
- preserve the resolved account identity on canonical postings.

Do not redesign DEC-11.

21. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE
==================================================

An unapplied payment, payment-on-account and overpayment must never be represented merely as a UI status if the underlying accounting requires a different Balance Sheet classification.

Do not create artificial journal entries merely to make a screen display a particular status.

Where accounting treatment is required, it must use the DEC-04 canonical journal.
