DEC-15 — REFUND POLICY
STATUS: APPROVED — product/accounting policy only

Approve the controlled refund model with the following boundaries:

1. CUSTOMER REFUNDS

Customer refunds may only be made from an available customer-credit liability or another explicitly approved refundable customer balance.

A refund must never exceed the currently available refundable balance.

Refund availability must be revalidated at approval and posting time.

2. SUPPLIER REFUND RECEIPTS

Supplier refund receipts may only be recognised against an available supplier-prepayment asset or another explicitly approved refundable supplier balance.

A supplier refund receipt must not create an asset that did not previously exist.

3. CREDIT NOTES

Use a credit note where a commercial or VAT correction requires one.

Do NOT create a credit note merely because a customer is receiving a pure overpayment refund.

Credit notes and refunds remain distinct processes.

DEC-03 remains the authoritative VAT policy.

4. DISTINCT PROCESSES

Keep the following distinct:

- payment reversal;
- allocation reversal;
- credit note;
- customer credit;
- supplier prepayment;
- refund;
- bank evidence.

Do not use refund functionality to conceal an accounting correction.

5. CUSTOMER REFUND ACCOUNTING

For a refund from an approved customer-credit liability, the canonical accounting treatment is:

DR Customer Credit / Receipt-on-Account Liability
CR Bank/Cash

Use the appropriate approved account mappings and DEC-11 configuration version.

6. SUPPLIER REFUND RECEIPT ACCOUNTING

For a supplier refund against an approved supplier-prepayment asset:

DR Bank/Cash
CR Supplier Prepayment / Receivable Asset

Use the appropriate approved account mappings and DEC-11 configuration version.

7. REFUND LIMITS

A refund must never exceed the currently available refundable balance.

Revalidate the balance immediately before consequential approval/posting.

Prevent negative customer-credit or supplier-prepayment balances caused by an invalid refund.

8. REFUND WORKFLOW

Use explicit controlled states for the refund lifecycle.

At minimum support the necessary distinction between:

- requested;
- approved;
- posted;
- completed;
- failed;
- reversed/corrected.

Do not introduce unnecessary states.

A refund must not be treated as completed merely because a request exists.

9. REFUND APPROVAL

Refunds are consequential financial actions.

Use the DEC-05 server-side capability model.

Require elevated approval for consequential refunds.

For higher-risk refunds, apply appropriate separation of duties.

Do not assume every refund requires dual approval if the risk does not justify it.

10. BANK RECONCILIATION

A refund posting is canonical accounting.

Bank evidence may subsequently be reconciled to the existing refund posting.

Reconciliation must NEVER create duplicate refund accounting.

Maintain the distinction between:

BANK EVIDENCE
and
CANONICAL REFUND POSTING.

11. VAT

Pure overpayment refunds do not themselves create VAT.

VAT effects remain governed by the approved DEC-03 document/credit-note treatment.

Do not create a second VAT engine.

Where a commercial/VAT correction requires a credit note, use the approved credit-note accounting/VAT process rather than treating the refund itself as the VAT event.

12. PAYMENT REVERSAL

A payment reversal is not automatically a refund.

If an accounting payment was erroneous, use the appropriate canonical correction/reversal mechanism.

Do not use a customer refund to conceal or correct an erroneous accounting payment.

13. ALLOCATION REVERSAL

Reversing an allocation is not automatically a refund.

Allocation changes must follow DEC-12 and DEC-14 append-only allocation history.

A resulting customer credit may subsequently become refundable under this DEC-15 policy if it is an approved refundable balance.

14. REFUND CANCELLATION

A refund request that has not yet created canonical accounting may be cancelled subject to appropriate authority.

Once canonical accounting has been posted, do not delete the refund.

Use controlled reversal/correction mechanisms.

15. REFUND FAILURE

If the external/bank refund fails after the accounting process has begun, do not silently delete the accounting history.

The failure must be recorded and the accounting state corrected through an explicit controlled process.

Do not mark a failed refund as completed.

16. REFUND REVERSALS

A completed refund that must be corrected must be corrected through an explicit canonical reversal/correction.

Never:

- delete the original refund;
- rewrite the original journal;
- silently restore the previous balance.

Preserve immutable history.

17. PERIOD CONTROLS

Refund accounting must comply with DEC-07.

Refund posting must use a valid posting date and open accounting period.

Refunds must never bypass closed-period controls.

Where correction requires a closed-period action, use the approved controlled correction/reopen mechanisms rather than rewriting history.

18. CONFIGURATION

Refund accounting must respect DEC-10 and DEC-11.

Where material account configuration affects the refund:

- resolve the applicable configuration version;
- use the approved mapping;
- record resolved account IDs on the canonical posting.

19. REPORTING

Refunds must remain correctly represented in:

- customer statements;
- supplier statements;
- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- AR/AP reporting;
- cash/bank reporting;
- Profit & Loss where the underlying accounting genuinely affects it.

Reports must derive from canonical accounting.

20. AUDIT

Refund activity must retain immutable audit evidence covering, where applicable:

- requester;
- approver;
- company;
- refund amount;
- refundable source balance;
- reason;
- timestamps;
- approval;
- posting;
- completion/failure;
- reversal/correction;
- bank reconciliation relationship.

21. PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT BOUNDARY

DEC-16 remains responsible for the user-facing payment-on-account launch scope.

DEC-15 does NOT approve or reject payment-on-account.

Where payment-on-account is subsequently approved, its refund interaction must remain consistent with DEC-15's refundable-balance principles.

22. MIGRATION

Historical refunds, customer credits, supplier prepayments and related accounting may only be migrated where supported by evidence.

Never invent:

- historical refunds;
- refund dates;
- refund approvals;
- historical refundable balances;
- refund relationships.

Ambiguity becomes an explicit DEC-22 migration exception.

23. NO SILENT AUTOMATION

Do NOT enable silent automatic refund execution at launch.

Future refund automation requires a separate approved policy and must remain consistent with the Ledgerly Manifesto:

AI may assist and recommend.

Rules determine accounting validity.

Users remain in control of consequential financial actions.

24. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The approved model must not unnecessarily prevent future:

- payment-on-account refunds;
- additional refund methods;
- automated refund workflows;
- additional currencies;
- additional markets;
- additional banking integrations;
- configurable approval thresholds.
