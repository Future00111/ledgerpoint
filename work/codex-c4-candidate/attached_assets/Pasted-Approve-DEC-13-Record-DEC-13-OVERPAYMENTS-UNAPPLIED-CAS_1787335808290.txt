DEC-13 — OVERPAYMENTS, UNAPPLIED CASH AND EXCESS PAYMENT POLICY
STATUS: APPROVED

Approve the recommended first-class party-credit/prepayment model with the following boundaries:

1. NO LOSS OR CAP OF EXCESS MONEY

Ledgerpoint must never discard, cap, or silently absorb excess payment amounts.

Any genuine excess must remain represented and traceable.

2. CUSTOMER EXCESS

A customer payment exceeding the amount that can currently be allocated must be represented as a:

CUSTOMER CREDIT / RECEIPT-ON-ACCOUNT LIABILITY

Do not make an invoice or customer AR balance silently become negative merely because a customer has paid too much.

3. SUPPLIER EXCESS

A supplier payment exceeding the amount that can currently be allocated must be represented as a:

SUPPLIER PREPAYMENT / RECEIVABLE ASSET

Do not make a supplier bill or AP balance silently become negative merely because the business has paid too much.

4. KNOWN-PARTY PAYMENT WITHOUT DOCUMENT

Ledgerpoint may record a known-party accounting payment before a specific invoice or bill has been identified.

Do not invent a fake invoice or bill merely to provide somewhere to allocate the payment.

5. BANK EVIDENCE

Unmatched bank evidence remains distinct from a known-party accounting payment.

A bank-feed transaction awaiting reconciliation must not automatically become a party credit/prepayment simply because it has not yet been allocated.

6. ALLOCATION LIMIT

Allocation must not exceed:

- the remaining eligible payment amount; or
- the eligible document open amount.

Do not allow allocation logic to create negative invoice or bill balances.

7. CUSTOMER CREDIT ALLOCATION

When an existing customer credit is later allocated to an eligible customer invoice, the accounting treatment must use a controlled canonical reclassification:

DR Customer Credit / Receipt-on-Account Liability
CR Accounts Receivable

The original cash receipt/payment accounting must NOT be reposted merely because the credit is later allocated.

8. SUPPLIER PREPAYMENT ALLOCATION

When a supplier prepayment is later allocated to an eligible supplier bill, the accounting treatment must use a controlled canonical reclassification:

DR Accounts Payable
CR Supplier Prepayment / Receivable Asset

The original cash payment accounting must NOT be reposted merely because the prepayment is later allocated.

9. DIRECT PAYMENT REALLOCATION

Where a payment has already been posted and its allocation changes between eligible documents:

- preserve append-only allocation history;
- do not repost the original cash/AR/AP entry merely because the allocation changed;
- use the appropriate controlled allocation/reclassification mechanism where accounting treatment genuinely changes.

10. AR / AP PRESENTATION

Customer credits must remain distinct from ordinary aged receivables.

Supplier prepayments must remain distinct from ordinary aged payables.

Do not allow party-credit/prepayment balances to distort:

- aged receivables;
- aged payables;
- invoice balances;
- bill balances.

11. SETTLEMENT

DEC-12 remains authoritative for settlement.

Settlement status must continue to be derived from authoritative payment, allocation, open-item and accounting data.

Customer credits and supplier prepayments must not be treated as ordinary unpaid invoices/bills.

12. VAT

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Overpayments, customer credits and supplier prepayments must not create a second VAT engine.

VAT treatment remains document-driven under the approved DEC-03 policy unless a later approved decision explicitly changes that scope.

13. PERIOD CONTROLS

All canonical reclassification/accounting entries remain subject to DEC-07 accounting-period controls.

Do not allow credit/prepayment allocation or reclassification to bypass closed-period restrictions.

14. CONFIGURATION

Where a canonical reclassification requires material account configuration:

- resolve the applicable DEC-11 configuration version;
- use the approved DEC-10 control mappings;
- retain the resolved account identities on the canonical posting.

15. PERMISSIONS

Creation, allocation, reallocation and clearing of material customer-credit or supplier-prepayment balances must use the DEC-05 server-side capability model.

Do not rely on frontend permissions or arbitrary role strings.

16. AUDIT

Maintain immutable audit history for:

- excess payment identification;
- customer-credit creation;
- supplier-prepayment creation;
- allocation;
- reallocation;
- credit/prepayment application;
- adjustments;
- clearing actions.

17. MIGRATION

Historical excess payments, customer credits, supplier prepayments and allocations may only be migrated where supported by evidence.

Never invent:

- historical excess;
- historical credit balances;
- historical prepayments;
- historical allocations;
- historical dates;
- historical accounting treatment.

Ambiguous historical information becomes an explicit DEC-22 migration exception.

18. DEC-14 / DEC-15 / DEC-16 BOUNDARY

DEC-13 establishes the underlying accounting representation.

It does NOT approve the detailed policies for:

- first-class unapplied-cash workflow;
- refund workflow;
- refund source;
- refund VAT treatment;
- refund approval;
- payment-on-account user-facing launch scope.

Those remain for DEC-14, DEC-15 and DEC-16 respectively.

19. SOURCE FRESHNESS

The separately identified source-freshness/posting-safety dependency remains unresolved.

Do NOT absorb it into DEC-13.

20. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The architecture should support future:

- customer credit workflows;
- supplier prepayment workflows;
- refunds;
- payment-on-account;
- automated allocation;
- additional currencies;
- additional markets;
- additional payment methods.
