DEC-20 — BACKUP AND RECOVERY OBJECTIVES
STATUS: APPROVED — product/operational resilience policy only

Approve the managed, encrypted and tested backup/recovery model with the following boundaries.

1. BACKUP SCOPE

Protect, as appropriate:

- canonical accounting;
- operational accounting records;
- authoritative documents;
- configuration;
- audit records;
- retention/disposal state;
- other data required to reconstruct an authorised service state.

Distinguish authoritative data from rebuildable derived data.

Do not require permanent backup retention for data that can safely and deterministically be rebuilt unless another approved requirement applies.

2. BACKUP MODEL

Use a managed backup model incorporating, where supported and operationally appropriate:

- managed database snapshots;
- incremental/differential protection;
- transaction-log/WAL protection;
- point-in-time recovery;
- appropriate document/object-storage protection.

The exact implementation remains an engineering decision within the approved policy boundaries.

3. EXPORT DISTINCTION

Backups are NOT DEC-19 exports.

Exports are user-facing authorised copies/representations.

Backups are resilience infrastructure.

Do not expose backups as ordinary exports or use exports as the primary backup mechanism.

4. RECOVERY MODEL

Restore into an isolated recovery environment by default.

Never silently overwrite live accounting data.

Before promotion or restoration into a live service state, validate as appropriate:

- canonical journals;
- source links;
- accounting integrity;
- idempotency;
- accounting periods;
- financial-year assignments;
- configuration;
- VAT;
- payment/allocation/settlement;
- documents;
- audit history;
- retention/disposal state;
- company scope.

5. ACCOUNTING IMMUTABILITY

Recovery must preserve DEC-04.

Do NOT use backup restoration to:

- erase accounting errors;
- rewrite posted journals;
- alter historical VAT;
- change AR/AP history;
- alter financial-year assignment;
- bypass closed periods;
- remove audit history.

An accounting correction must use the approved accounting correction/reversal mechanisms.

Recovery is a resilience mechanism, not an accounting correction mechanism.

6. POINT-IN-TIME RECOVERY

Support point-in-time recovery where operationally available and appropriate.

Point-in-time recovery must represent restoration to an authorised historical system state.

It must not silently become a mechanism for rewriting canonical accounting history.

7. RECOVERY TARGETS

Adopt the following as TARGET OBJECTIVES, subject to operational validation:

| Data tier | Target RPO | Target RTO |
|---|---:|---:|
| Canonical accounting and controls | ≤ 1 hour | ≤ 4 hours |
| Operational accounting records | ≤ 4 hours | ≤ 8 hours |
| Authoritative documents | ≤ 24 hours | ≤ 24 hours, or the stricter related accounting target |
| Rebuildable derived data | Best effort | ≤ 24 hours |

These are policy targets, not guaranteed service-level commitments.

They must be validated against the actual infrastructure and operating model before implementation.

8. BACKUP RETENTION

Backup retention must be designed consistently with DEC-17 and DEC-18 while remaining distinct from production-data retention.

Do not automatically assume backup retention must equal production retention.

Backup expiry must be controlled, auditable and compatible with applicable legal holds and recovery requirements.

9. DELETION / ANONYMISATION

Respect DEC-18.

Do not use backups to permanently preserve production information that should legitimately disappear.

At the same time, do not require destructive modification of immutable backup media merely to imitate production deletion where an approved lifecycle/expiry mechanism is more appropriate.

The detailed interaction between production disposal and backup lifecycle must remain consistent with DEC-18 and operationally validated.

10. SECURITY

Backups must use appropriate:

- encryption at rest;
- encryption in transit;
- separate credentials;
- access controls;
- key management;
- integrity verification;
- administrative separation;
- immutable/WORM protection where appropriate.

11. COMPANY SCOPE / ISOLATION

Recovery must never expose one company's data to another company.

Company scope must be enforced server-side.

Detailed tenant-isolation architecture remains DEC-21.

12. RECOVERY AUTHORITY

Recovery is a high-risk operation.

Use controlled server-side authority.

Where appropriate require:

- elevated capability;
- explicit authorisation;
- approval;
- separation of duties.

Do not rely solely on frontend controls.

13. RESTORATION

Default to isolated recovery.

Do not silently overwrite live accounting.

Where a recovery must affect a live service state, require explicit controlled promotion/restoration authority and validation.

Record the recovery event immutably.

14. BACKUP VERIFICATION

Backups must be automatically checked where practical.

A backup that cannot be reliably restored is not considered an effective recovery control.

15. RESTORE TESTING

Perform representative restore tests at least quarterly.

Where supported, perform a whole-service or regional recovery exercise at least annually.

Record the results and any material failures.

These are operational policy expectations and should be validated against the final infrastructure.

16. FAILURE HANDLING

Backup/recovery failures must be detectable and escalated appropriately.

This includes:

- backup failure;
- integrity-verification failure;
- storage failure;
- recovery failure;
- unavailable point-in-time recovery;
- failed restore test.

Do not silently treat an unhealthy backup state as healthy.

17. AUDIT

Maintain immutable audit evidence for:

- backup creation;
- backup verification;
- backup retention/expiry;
- recovery initiation;
- recovery approval;
- restoration;
- promotion;
- recovery testing;
- legal holds where relevant;
- material recovery failures.

18. USER EXPERIENCE

Normal users should see only appropriate business-level information such as:

- backup/recovery health where relevant;
- recovery readiness;
- material incidents affecting their company.

Do not expose unnecessary infrastructure implementation details.

Developer/infrastructure diagnostics remain outside the normal customer experience.

19. MIGRATION

DEC-22 remains responsible for migration/cutover policy.

DEC-20 may provide recovery safeguards such as:

- pre-cutover snapshots;
- rollback points;
- recovery copies;
- preservation of legacy evidence.

Do not use DEC-20 to decide the migration strategy itself.

20. TENANT ISOLATION

DEC-21 remains responsible for the detailed tenant-isolation policy.

DEC-20 nevertheless requires recovery mechanisms to preserve strict company isolation.

21. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The policy must allow future improvements such as:

- multi-region recovery;
- improved RPO/RTO;
- additional backup tiers;
- additional storage providers;
- regulatory requirements;
- advanced disaster recovery;
- additional markets.

Material policy changes follow the Living Product Decisions process.
