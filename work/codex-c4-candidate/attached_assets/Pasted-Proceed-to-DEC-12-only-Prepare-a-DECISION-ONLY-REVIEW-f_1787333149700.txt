==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine Ledgerly's authoritative policy for:

- payments;
- payment allocation;
- settlement;
- partially paid invoices/bills;
- fully paid invoices/bills;
- unapplied payments;
- overpayments;
- payment-on-account where applicable;
- allocation changes.

The review must clearly separate:

1. PAYMENT EVIDENCE
2. PAYMENT ACCOUNTING
3. PAYMENT ALLOCATION
4. DOCUMENT SETTLEMENT STATUS

These must not be treated as the same thing.

==================================================
1. PAYMENT AS AN ACCOUNTING CONCEPT
==================================================

Define what a payment represents in the canonical accounting model.

Determine whether a payment should be treated as:

- a standalone accounting event;
- an allocation event;
- both, with separate evidence and accounting effects;
- another controlled model.

Respect DEC-04's separation of:

- bank evidence;
- payment;
- allocation;
- canonical journal.

==================================================
2. CUSTOMER PAYMENTS
==================================================

Define the accounting model for customer payments.

At minimum consider:

BANK/CASH → ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE

Explain how this affects:

- customer balance;
- invoice balance;
- aged receivables;
- bank reconciliation;
- reporting.

==================================================
3. SUPPLIER PAYMENTS
==================================================

Define the accounting model for supplier payments.

At minimum consider:

ACCOUNTS PAYABLE → BANK/CASH

Explain how this affects:

- supplier balance;
- bill balance;
- aged payables;
- bank reconciliation;
- reporting.

==================================================
4. ALLOCATION
==================================================

Define payment allocation.

Determine:

- whether a payment can exist before allocation;
- whether allocation creates accounting postings;
- whether allocation changes accounting or only settlement state;
- whether allocation is reversible;
- how allocation is audited.

A payment allocation must not accidentally create duplicate accounting entries.

==================================================
5. PARTIAL PAYMENTS
==================================================

Define treatment of:

- partially paid customer invoices;
- partially paid supplier bills;
- multiple payments against one document;
- one payment against multiple documents.

Explain how outstanding balances are calculated.

==================================================
6. FULL SETTLEMENT
==================================================

Define when an invoice/bill becomes:

- partially paid;
- fully paid;
- overpaid.

Settlement status must be derived from authoritative payment/allocation data rather than being an independently editable accounting fact.

==================================================
7. UNAPPLIED PAYMENTS
==================================================

Determine the launch policy for payments that have been received/paid but are not yet allocated.

Consider:

- customer payment received with no invoice identified;
- supplier payment made with no bill identified;
- payment held on account;
- bank transaction awaiting classification.

Explain the accounting and reporting consequences.

Do not resolve DEC-13 through DEC-16 beyond their dependencies.

==================================================
8. OVERPAYMENTS
==================================================

Identify the correct accounting model for:

- customer overpayment;
- supplier overpayment.

Determine whether the excess amount becomes:

- unapplied/on-account balance;
- separate liability/asset treatment;
- another controlled accounting state.

Do not approve DEC-13/14/15/16 policies through DEC-12.

Identify which parts must remain for those later decisions.

==================================================
9. PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT
==================================================

Determine whether Ledgerly should support payment-on-account at launch.

If recommended, explain:

- accounting treatment;
- customer/supplier balance;
- allocation later;
- refunds;
- reporting;
- audit.

Clearly distinguish payment-on-account from an ordinary bank transaction awaiting reconciliation.

==================================================
10. ALLOCATION CHANGES
==================================================

Determine what happens when an existing allocation is changed.

Examples:

- payment allocated to Invoice A, then moved to Invoice B;
- payment partially allocated, then reallocated;
- payment becomes unapplied;
- overpayment is subsequently allocated.

Determine whether:

- settlement state is recalculated;
- accounting journals change;
- reversal/reposting is required;
- allocation history is preserved.

Do not allow silent historical mutation.

==================================================

==================================================
26. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Payment, allocation and settlement are related but distinct concepts.

Do not allow:

- allocation to duplicate accounting;
- settlement status to become an independently editable accounting fact;
- bank evidence to be mistaken for an accounting payment;
- reallocation to silently rewrite historical accounting.

Where accounting must change, use the canonical posting/reversal mechanisms approved under DEC-04.
