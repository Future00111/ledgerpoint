DEC-07 — ACCOUNTING-PERIOD POLICY

STATUS: APPROVED

1. PERIOD FREQUENCY

Ledgerpoint will use contiguous MONTHLY accounting periods.

Periods are anchored to each company's DEC-06 financial year.

The monthly period structure must work correctly for:
- 1 April–31 March financial years;
- alternative valid financial-year starts permitted by DEC-06;
- first/partial financial years;
- normal recurring financial years.

2. PERIOD GENERATION

All known periods for a financial year should be generated automatically.

Future periods may exist before they become active for posting.

Do not require users to manually create every accounting period.

3. PERIOD STATES

The authoritative accounting-period lifecycle will use only:

OPEN
CLOSED

Do not introduce additional accounting-period states unless a future, explicitly approved decision requires them.

4. PERIOD ASSIGNMENT

The accounting period for a canonical posting is determined server-side from the validated POSTING DATE.

Do not allow the browser/client to choose an arbitrary accounting period.

Do not silently move a transaction into another period if its posting date does not belong to an appropriate open period.

5. CLOSED PERIODS

A closed period must prevent ordinary accounting postings.

There must be no direct posting bypass around a closed period.

If a correction genuinely belongs to a closed period, the supported mechanisms are:

- controlled privileged reopening; or
- an appropriately approved adjustment in a later open period.

The exact accounting treatment remains subject to the relevant accounting rules and later decisions.

6. PERIOD CLOSE

Closing a period is a consequential accounting action.

It must use the DEC-05 capability model and require:

- active company membership;
- company scope;
- server-side capability enforcement;
- validation;
- appropriate authority;
- audit evidence.

The system should perform close validation before allowing the period to close.

7. CLOSE WARNINGS VS BLOCKERS

The following should normally be VISIBLE WARNINGS rather than automatic close blockers:

- draft transactions;
- unallocated payments;
- unreconciled bank evidence;
- ordinary VAT timetable differences.

However, genuine accounting or VAT integrity failures must remain capable of blocking close.

Do not invent additional mandatory blockers without an explicit product/accounting decision.

Warnings should clearly explain:
- what is outstanding;
- why it matters;
- what action could resolve it.

8. PERIOD REOPEN

Reopening a closed period is a highly consequential accounting action.

It must require:

- elevated capability;
- active company membership;
- company scope;
- server-side enforcement;
- mandatory reason;
- audit trail.

Where segregation of duties requires it, reopening should require separate approval.

Do not allow a normal role to bypass the reopen capability simply because it can otherwise post accounting entries.

9. NO HISTORY REWRITING

Changing period configuration must never silently rewrite or reclassify posted accounting history.

The DEC-04 principles remain authoritative:

- posted journals are immutable;
- source linkage is retained;
- corrections/reversals are explicit;
- audit history is preserved.

10. PERIOD / OTHER CALENDAR DISTINCTION

Accounting periods must remain distinct from:

- VAT periods;
- tax years;
- bank statement periods;
- calendar months as a general date concept;
- financial-year identity.

A calendar month may coincide with an accounting period, but the accounting-period identity remains a company-specific accounting construct.

11. REPORTING

Period state and identity must support authoritative:

- Profit & Loss;
- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- comparative reporting;
- period-based accounting analysis.

Reports must continue to derive from the DEC-04 canonical accounting model.

12. MIGRATION

Historical period assignment must use available evidence.

Ledgerpoint must NEVER invent:

- historical periods;
- historical close events;
- historical journals;
- accounting dates;
- accounting facts.

Where historical period information is incomplete or ambiguous, preserve that limitation and handle it explicitly through the later migration decision.

13. DEC-05 INTEGRATION

Period creation, close and reopen must use the approved DEC-05 capability architecture.

Do not hard-code authority based solely on role names.

Server-side capability checks remain authoritative.

14. DEC-06 / DEC-08 BOUNDARY

DEC-07 depends on the financial-year structure approved by DEC-06.

DEC-07 does NOT approve or resolve DEC-08.

DEC-08 remains responsible for year-end mechanics, including any year-end closing treatment and retained-earnings behaviour.

15. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The period architecture must not unnecessarily prevent:

- alternative financial-year starts already permitted by DEC-06;
- future markets;
- future currencies;
- future accounting/reporting requirements;
- future amendments to period policy.

Any future change must follow the Living Product Decisions process.
