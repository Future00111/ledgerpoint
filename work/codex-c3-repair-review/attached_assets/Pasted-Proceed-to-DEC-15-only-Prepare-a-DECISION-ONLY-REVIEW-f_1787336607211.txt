==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine Ledgerly's policy for refunds.

The review must clearly distinguish:

- refund;
- payment reversal;
- credit note;
- customer credit;
- supplier prepayment;
- unapplied cash;
- allocation reversal;
- bank evidence.

Determine the appropriate accounting and product treatment for refunds while preserving the DEC-04 canonical journal model.

==================================================
1. REFUND DEFINITION
==================================================

Define precisely what Ledgerly considers a refund.

Distinguish refunds from:

- reversing an incorrect payment;
- reversing an allocation;
- issuing a credit note;
- leaving a customer credit;
- returning a supplier prepayment;
- correcting a bank reconciliation error.

==================================================
2. CUSTOMER REFUNDS
==================================================

Determine the policy for returning money to a customer.

Consider:

- refund of a settled invoice;
- refund of a partially paid invoice;
- refund of a customer credit;
- refund of an overpayment;
- refund following a credit note;
- refund where no invoice exists;
- refund of a payment-on-account balance if DEC-16 later approves it.

Do not assume every scenario must be supported at launch.

==================================================
3. SUPPLIER REFUNDS
==================================================

Determine the policy for money returned by a supplier.

Consider:

- supplier refund against a bill;
- supplier refund of an overpayment;
- supplier refund of a supplier prepayment;
- refund following a supplier credit note.

Clearly distinguish supplier refunds from customer refunds.

==================================================
4. REFUND SOURCE
==================================================

Determine where the refund originates from.

Examples:

- bank/cash account;
- customer credit balance;
- supplier prepayment balance;
- another approved accounting source.

Do not allow a refund to create money that did not previously exist in the accounting model.

==================================================
5. ACCOUNTING TREATMENT
==================================================

Determine the canonical accounting treatment.

For customer refunds, evaluate appropriate treatment such as:

DR Customer Credit / relevant liability
CR Bank/Cash

For supplier refunds, evaluate appropriate treatment such as:

DR Bank/Cash
CR Supplier Prepayment / relevant asset

Do not assume these examples are automatically correct in every circumstance.

Explain the treatment for each supported scenario.

==================================================
6. REFUND VS CREDIT NOTE
==================================================

Define the relationship between:

- credit note;
- customer credit;
- refund.

Determine whether:

- a credit note can create a customer credit;
- a credit can be refunded;
- a refund requires a credit note;
- a refund can exist without a credit note.

Respect DEC-03 and existing invoice/credit-note policy.

==================================================
7. REFUND VS PAYMENT REVERSAL
==================================================

Define the distinction between:

- correcting an erroneous payment;
- reversing a legitimate payment;
- refunding a customer;
- refunding a supplier.

Do not allow a user to use refund functionality to conceal an accounting correction.

==================================================
8. REFUND APPROVAL
==================================================

Determine:

- who can initiate a refund;
- who can approve a refund;
- whether separate approval is required;
- whether thresholds should exist;
- whether approval requirements vary by amount or risk.

Use DEC-05 capability principles.

Do not silently grant authority.

==================================================
9. REFUND WORKFLOW
==================================================

Define the user workflow.

Consider:

1. identify refundable balance;
2. select refund amount;
3. select source/account;
4. validate;
5. approve where required;
6. create canonical accounting;
7. record refund;
8. reconcile resulting bank transaction.

Determine whether refunds should be:

- immediate;
- scheduled;
- pending;
- completed;
- failed.

Avoid introducing unnecessary states.

==================================================
10. REFUND AMOUNT LIMITS
==================================================

Determine whether users can refund:

- less than available credit;
- exactly available credit;
- more than available credit.

If over-refunding is prohibited, define how the system prevents it.

Do not allow negative customer credits or supplier prepayments merely to facilitate a refund.

==================================================
11. REFUND CANCELLATION / FAILURE
==================================================

Determine what happens when:

- a refund is cancelled before posting;
- a bank payment fails;
- a refund is reversed;
- a refund was created incorrectly.

Ensure accounting history remains immutable.

==================================================
12. REFUND REVERSALS
==================================================

Determine how an incorrectly completed refund is corrected.

Do not delete or rewrite the original refund journal.

Use explicit canonical reversal/correction mechanisms.

==================================================
13. VAT
==================================================

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Determine how refunds interact with VAT only within the existing approved VAT model.

Do not create new VAT rules.

Do not create a second VAT engine.

Clearly identify any VAT questions that belong outside DEC-15.

==================================================
14. CREDIT NOTES
==================================================

Explain how refunds interact with:

- customer credit notes;
- supplier credit notes.

Do not redefine the entire credit-note policy if it belongs elsewhere.

Identify dependencies.

==================================================
15. UNAPPLIED CASH / CUSTOMER CREDIT
==================================================

Respect DEC-13 and DEC-14.

Determine the hand-off from:

CUSTOMER CREDIT
→ REFUND

and:

SUPPLIER PREPAYMENT
→ REFUND

without conflating refunds with allocation.

==================================================
16. PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT
==================================================

DEC-16 remains responsible for payment-on-account launch scope.

Do not approve payment-on-account through DEC-15.

Where relevant, identify how a future payment-on-account balance could interact with refunds.

==================================================
17. PERIOD CONTROLS
==================================================

Respect DEC-07.

Refund accounting must:

- use the correct posting date;
- respect open/closed periods;
- never bypass closed-period controls.

==================================================
18. CONFIGURATION
==================================================

Respect DEC-10 and DEC-11.

Where refund accounting requires material account configuration:

- resolve the applicable configuration version;
- use approved mappings;
- retain resolved account IDs on canonical postings.

==================================================
19. BANK RECONCILIATION
==================================================

Explain how a refund interacts with:

- outgoing bank payment;
- incoming supplier refund;
- bank evidence;
- reconciliation.

Bank evidence must remain distinct from canonical accounting.

Reconciliation must not create duplicate refund accounting.

==================================================
20. REPORTING
==================================================

Determine how refunds appear in:

- customer statements;
- supplier statements;
- Balance Sheet;
- Profit & Loss where relevant;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- AR/AP reporting;
- cash/bank reporting.

Reports must remain derived from canonical accounting data.

==================================================
21. AUDIT
==================================================

Define audit requirements for:

- refund request;
- approval;
- posting;
- bank reconciliation;
- cancellation;
- failure;
- reversal;
- correction.

Audit history must remain immutable.

==================================================
22. PERMISSIONS
==================================================

Use DEC-05.

Determine appropriate capabilities for:

- initiate refund;
- approve refund;
- post refund;
- reverse/correct refund.

Consider whether separation of duties is appropriate for higher-risk refunds.

==================================================
23. MIGRATION
==================================================

Review migration treatment for historical:

- refunds;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refund relationships;
- credit notes.

Never invent historical refunds, dates, approvals or accounting relationships.

Ambiguity becomes an explicit DEC-22 migration exception.

==================================================
27. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE
==================================================

A refund must represent a genuine movement of money or controlled accounting correction.

Do not create a refund merely to change a screen balance.

Do not use refund functionality to hide an accounting correction.

Refunds must use the DEC-04 canonical journal model.

Historical refunds must remain immutable.
