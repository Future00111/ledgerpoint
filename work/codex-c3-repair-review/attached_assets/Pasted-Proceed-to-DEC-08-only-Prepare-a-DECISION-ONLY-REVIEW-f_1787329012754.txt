==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine the appropriate year-end accounting policy for Ledgerpoint.

The policy must explain how Ledgerpoint transitions from one approved DEC-06 financial year to the next while preserving the DEC-04 accounting source of truth.

The design must NOT rewrite, delete, mutate, or reclassify historical posted journals simply because a financial year has ended.

==================================================
1. YEAR-END MODEL
==================================================

Evaluate the realistic approaches for year-end treatment, including where applicable:

- no physical closing journal, with reporting determining retained earnings;
- explicit year-end closing journal;
- a hybrid approach.

Explain the accounting and technical consequences of each.

Recommend the approach that best fits Ledgerpoint's canonical journal architecture.

==================================================
2. PROFIT AND LOSS
==================================================

Define how income and expense accounts behave across financial-year boundaries.

Determine how Ledgerpoint should represent:

- current-year profit/loss;
- prior-year results;
- comparative reporting;
- retained earnings/equity.

Do not allow the reporting layer to create accounting facts that do not exist in the canonical journal.

==================================================
3. RETAINED EARNINGS
==================================================

Define how Ledgerpoint should handle retained earnings between financial years.

Clarify whether retained earnings should be:

- represented through explicit accounting postings;
- represented through reporting/read-model logic;
- or handled through another controlled approach.

If an explicit journal is recommended, define:

- source;
- posting date;
- accounts affected;
- audit relationship;
- idempotency;
- reversal/correction behaviour;
- year-end linkage.

==================================================
4. BALANCE SHEET
==================================================

Explain how Balance Sheet accounts behave at year-end.

Clearly distinguish:

PERMANENT ACCOUNTS
from
TEMPORARY ACCOUNTS.

Explain how:

- assets;
- liabilities;
- equity;
- revenue;
- cost of sales;
- expenses

behave across financial-year boundaries.

==================================================
5. TRIAL BALANCE
==================================================

Define how the Trial Balance should behave:

- before year-end;
- at year-end;
- after the next financial year begins;
- when historical periods are queried.

Ensure the canonical journal remains authoritative.

==================================================
6. GENERAL LEDGER
==================================================

Define how the General Ledger should represent:

- historical financial years;
- year-end boundaries;
- retained earnings;
- any year-end adjustment or closing entries.

Historical journals must remain visible and immutable.

==================================================
7. YEAR-END PROCESS
==================================================

Define the intended year-end workflow.

Consider:

- validation;
- outstanding drafts;
- unresolved accounting issues;
- VAT issues;
- unreconciled banking;
- unallocated payments;
- period state;
- final reporting;
- year-end approval;
- audit record;
- creation of the next financial year.

Do not automatically turn operational warnings into blockers without justification.

==================================================
8. YEAR-END APPROVAL
==================================================

Determine:

- who can perform year-end;
- who can approve it;
- whether separate approval is required;
- which DEC-05 capability is required;
- whether segregation of duties applies.

Year-end must be server-side, company-scoped and auditable.

==================================================
9. YEAR-END REVERSALS / CORRECTIONS
==================================================

Determine how Ledgerpoint handles a correction discovered after year-end.

Examples:

- an expense was posted incorrectly;
- an invoice was omitted;
- a VAT correction is discovered;
- an accounting error is discovered in a previous year.

Explain whether the appropriate treatment is:

- reopening the previous period;
- posting a correction in the current period;
- creating a controlled prior-period adjustment;
- another approach.

Do not resolve policies belonging to later decisions unless they are necessary to explain the year-end model.

==================================================
10. CLOSED YEARS
==================================================

Distinguish:

- closed accounting period;
- completed financial year;
- year-end process completed;
- historical financial year.

Determine whether a completed financial year requires a separate status or whether existing period/accounting structures are sufficient.

Avoid unnecessary status complexity.

==================================================
11. MIGRATION
==================================================

Explain how historical year-end information should be migrated.

Never invent:

- closing journals;
- retained earnings;
- historical approvals;
- year-end events;
- accounting facts.

Where evidence is incomplete, preserve that limitation.

==================================================
12. REPORTING
==================================================

Explain the impact on:

- Profit & Loss;
- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- comparative reports;
- retained earnings reporting.

Reports must remain derived from the DEC-04 canonical accounting model.

==================================================
13. VAT BOUNDARY
==================================================

DEC-03 remains authoritative for VAT.

Do not allow year-end treatment to alter VAT calculations or create a second VAT engine.

Explain how year-end accounting interacts with VAT without expanding DEC-03 scope.

==================================================
14. OPTIONS

Present all realistic year-end approaches.

For each option explain:

- accounting consequences;
- data/schema consequences;
- migration consequences;
- reporting consequences;
- audit/security consequences;
- operational consequences;
- compatibility consequences;
- future flexibility;
- what becomes difficult to change later.

==================================================
15. RECOMMENDATION

Provide a clearly labelled:

RECOMMENDATION — NOT APPROVAL

Recommend the approach that best satisfies:

- DEC-04 canonical accounting architecture;
- DEC-06 financial-year policy;
- DEC-07 period policy;
- auditability;
- accounting correctness;
- reporting correctness;
- historical immutability;
- future flexibility;
- practical usability for small businesses.

Do not convert the recommendation into an approval.

==================================================

==================================================

Clearly distinguish:

ALREADY DECIDED

DELIBERATELY LEFT OPEN

Explicitly identify:

- what approving DEC-08 would lock in;

- any policies that must remain separate.

==================================================
17. IMPORTANT ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLE
==================================================

The DEC-04 canonical journal is authoritative.

Year-end must NEVER be implemented by:

- deleting journal entries;
- rewriting posted journal lines;
- changing historical posting dates;
- silently changing financial-year assignments;
- overwriting historical balances.

If a year-end mechanism requires accounting entries, those entries must themselves be normal canonical, balanced, auditable, source-linked and idempotent postings.

==================================================
18. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY
==================================================

Do not unnecessarily prevent:

- alternative financial-year starts permitted by DEC-06;
- future markets;
- future currencies;
- future reporting requirements;
- future accounting policies.

Any future amendment must follow the Living Product Decisions process.
