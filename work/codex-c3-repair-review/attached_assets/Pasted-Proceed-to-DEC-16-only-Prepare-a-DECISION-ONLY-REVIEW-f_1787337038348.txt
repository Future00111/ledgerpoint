==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine whether Ledgerly should support USER-FACING PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT at launch.

DEC-13 already established the accounting representation for customer credits and supplier prepayments.

DEC-14 established the unapplied-cash workflow.

DEC-15 established refund policy.

DEC-16 must now decide the PRODUCT LAUNCH SCOPE and user-facing behaviour for payment-on-account.

Clearly distinguish:

- payment;
- unapplied payment;
- customer credit;
- supplier prepayment;
- payment-on-account;
- allocation;
- refund;
- credit note.

==================================================
1. DEFINITION
==================================================

Define precisely what Ledgerly means by:

PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT

Do not assume it is identical to:

- bank evidence;
- an unclassified payment;
- an unapplied payment;
- a customer credit;
- a supplier prepayment.

Explain the intended user/business meaning.

==================================================
2. CUSTOMER PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT
==================================================

Evaluate whether Ledgerly should allow a customer payment to be deliberately recorded as payment-on-account before an invoice exists or before allocation is known.

Consider:

- advance payment;
- customer deposit;
- retainer/prepayment;
- payment before invoice;
- credit balance.

Explain the accounting and user-facing consequences.

Respect DEC-13.

==================================================
3. SUPPLIER PAYMENT-ON-ACCOUNT
==================================================

Evaluate the equivalent supplier workflow.

Consider:

- supplier deposit;
- advance payment;
- payment before bill;
- supplier prepayment.

Explain the accounting and user-facing consequences.

==================================================
4. LAUNCH OPTIONS
==================================================

Present realistic options such as:

A. Do not expose payment-on-account at launch.
   Support the underlying accounting representation but keep it within unapplied/credit/prepayment workflows.

B. Support customer payment-on-account only.

C. Support both customer and supplier payment-on-account.

D. Another justified model.

For each option explain:

- accounting consequences;
- data/schema consequences;
- migration consequences;
- reporting consequences;
- VAT consequences;
- UX consequences;
- operational consequences;
- audit/security consequences;
- future flexibility;
- what becomes difficult to change later.

5. USER EXPERIENCE
==================================================

- where users create it;
- how it is labelled;
- how it differs from unapplied cash;
- how it appears on customer/supplier accounts;
- how it is later allocated;
- how it interacts with invoices/bills;
- how it can be refunded.

Ledgerly must use business language.

Do not expose accounting implementation details to normal users.

6. ACCOUNTING MODEL
==================================================

Respect DEC-13.

- a distinct user-facing state over the existing customer-credit/supplier-prepayment accounting;
- a distinct accounting object;
- another controlled representation.

Avoid duplicate accounting.

Do not create artificial journal entries merely to represent a UI label.

==================================================
7. ALLOCATION
==================================================

Respect DEC-12 and DEC-14.

Determine how a payment-on-account amount is later allocated.

For example:

Customer pays £1,000 on account.

Later Invoice A = £700.

£700 is allocated.

£300 remains on account.

Explain:

- accounting effect;
- settlement effect;
- remaining balance;
- audit history.

Do not repost the original cash receipt merely because allocation occurs.

==================================================
8. REFUNDS
==================================================

Determine how payment-on-account interacts with refunds.

- what approval is required;
- how the refund is represented;
- how accounting is posted.

Do not contradict DEC-15.
