==================================================
OBJECTIVE
==================================================

Determine the correct policy for Ledgerly's CONTROL-ACCOUNT MAPPINGS.

The purpose is to establish which Chart of Accounts accounts are authoritative for system-controlled accounting flows while preserving the DEC-09 principle that account names and codes are not accounting logic.

The review must distinguish between:

- account identity;
- account classification;
- control-account role;
- transaction/posting mapping;
- user-configurable accounts;
- system-protected accounts.

==================================================
1. CONTROL ACCOUNT MODEL
==================================================

Define what Ledgerly means by a control account.

Determine which accounts should be treated as system-controlled and why.

At minimum evaluate:

- Accounts Receivable
- Accounts Payable
- Bank/Cash
- VAT Output
- VAT Input
- VAT Control/Settlement
- Revenue
- Cost of Sales
- Expense
- Equity / retained-results presentation where relevant.

Do not assume every account listed above must be a control account.

Explain which genuinely require system-controlled treatment.

==================================================
2. AR MAPPING
==================================================

Determine the authoritative mapping for:

- customer invoice receivables;
- customer credit notes;
- customer payments;
- customer payment allocations;
- customer refunds where applicable.

Define whether AR must use a protected system account.

Explain how the mapping interacts with:

- invoices;
- payments;
- reconciliation;
- outstanding balances;
- aged receivables;
- reporting.

==================================================
3. AP MAPPING
==================================================

Determine the authoritative mapping for:

- supplier bills;
- supplier credit notes;
- supplier payments;
- supplier payment allocations;
- supplier refunds where applicable.

Define whether AP must use a protected system account.

Explain interaction with:

- bills;
- payments;
- outstanding balances;
- aged payables;
- reporting.

==================================================
4. BANK / CASH MAPPING
==================================================

Determine how Ledgerly maps bank and cash activity into accounting.

Distinguish between:

- bank-feed evidence;
- bank accounts;
- accounting cash/bank accounts;
- reconciliation;
- transfers.

Determine whether each connected accounting bank account should map to a protected account.

Do not confuse bank-feed evidence with canonical accounting postings.

Respect DEC-04's separation of:

- bank evidence;
- payment;
- allocation;
- accounting journal.

==================================================
5. VAT MAPPING
==================================================

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

Determine the required chart/control mappings for:

- output VAT;
- input VAT;
- VAT control/settlement;
- VAT adjustments;
- VAT return settlement/payment/refund where relevant.

Do not create a second VAT engine.

Ensure VAT postings remain traceable to DEC-03's deterministic VAT logic.

==================================================
6. SALES INVOICE MAPPING
==================================================

Determine the default accounting mapping for a normal sales invoice.

Consider:

- Accounts Receivable;
- Revenue;
- Output VAT.

Explain how the mapping behaves for:

- VATable sales;
- zero-rated sales;
- exempt sales;
- non-VAT transactions where supported.

Do not expand VAT scope beyond DEC-03.

==================================================
7. SUPPLIER BILL MAPPING
==================================================

Determine the default accounting mapping for a supplier bill.

Consider:

- Accounts Payable;
- Expense or Asset;
- Input VAT.

Explain how account selection should work without allowing users to bypass required control-account logic.

==================================================
8. PAYMENTS AND ALLOCATIONS
==================================================

Determine the mapping principles for:

CUSTOMER PAYMENT

BANK/CASH → AR

SUPPLIER PAYMENT

AP → BANK/CASH

Also explain how allocations interact with the canonical accounting model.

Do not resolve all payment edge cases belonging to DEC-12 through DEC-16 unless required to define the control-account architecture.

Clearly identify what remains for those later decisions.

==================================================
9. CREDIT NOTES
==================================================

Determine how credit notes should map to the appropriate control and underlying accounts.

Ensure credit notes do not create an alternative accounting path outside the canonical posting model.

==================================================
10. REFUNDS
==================================================

Identify the control-account implications of:

- customer refunds;
- supplier refunds.

Do not fully resolve DEC-14/DEC-15/DEC-16 payment/refund policy if those are separate decisions.

Identify the dependency clearly.

==================================================
11. USER CUSTOMISATION
==================================================

Determine what users may configure.

Consider:

- choosing revenue accounts;
- choosing expense accounts;
- choosing asset accounts;
- choosing bank accounts;
- changing AR;
- changing AP;
- changing VAT control accounts;
- deactivating mapped accounts;
- replacing mapped accounts.

Protected control-account mappings must not be changed in a way that silently changes historical accounting meaning.

==================================================
12. CONTROL-ACCOUNT PROTECTION
==================================================

Define:

- which mappings are protected;
- who may change them;
- whether changes require elevated capability;
- whether changes require validation;
- whether changes require audit evidence;
- whether changes take effect immediately or prospectively.

Respect DEC-05.

Do not grant permissions merely from role names.

==================================================
13. POSTED HISTORY
==================================================

Changing a control-account mapping must NEVER:

- rewrite posted journals;
- reclassify historical transactions;
- change historical VAT results;
- change historical AR/AP balances;
- alter historical reporting.

If a mapping changes, explain how future postings use the new mapping while historical postings retain their original accounting identity.

==================================================
14. CONFIGURATION VERSIONING
==================================================

Identify whether control-account mappings require versioning or effective dating.

Do not resolve the complete DEC-11 configuration-versioning policy.

Instead:

- identify the dependency;
- explain why versioning may be required;
- state what DEC-11 must decide.

==================================================
15. REPORTING
==================================================

Explain how control-account mappings support:

- Profit & Loss;
- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- aged receivables;
- aged payables;
- VAT reporting;
- bank reconciliation;
- payment reporting.

Reports must remain derived from the DEC-04 canonical journal model.

==================================================
16. MIGRATION
==================================================

Review how existing Ledgerpoint account mappings could be migrated.

Historical mappings must be evidence-based.

Never invent:

- AR mappings;
- AP mappings;
- VAT mappings;
- bank mappings;
- historical accounting relationships.

Where evidence is incomplete, record a migration exception for DEC-22.

==================================================
17. OPTIONS
==================================================

Present realistic options for:

- fixed system mappings;
- company-configurable mappings;
- protected mappings with controlled overrides;
- effective-dated mappings.

For each option explain:

- accounting consequences;
- data/schema consequences;
- migration consequences;
- reporting consequences;
- security/permission consequences;
- compatibility consequences;
- operational consequences;
- future flexibility;
- what becomes difficult to change later.

==================================================
18. RECOMMENDATION
==================================================

Provide:

RECOMMENDATION — NOT APPROVAL

Recommend the approach that best satisfies:

- DEC-04 canonical accounting;
- DEC-05 capability security;
- DEC-06 financial-year policy;
- DEC-07 period policy;
- DEC-08 reporting-only year-end;
- DEC-09 Chart of Accounts policy;
- DEC-03 VAT authority;
- usability for small businesses;
- accounting correctness;
- auditability;
- future flexibility.

Do not convert the recommendation into approval.

==================================================
20. IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE
==================================================

Control-account mappings are accounting configuration.

They must never become an uncontrolled way to alter historical accounting.

Stable account identity and canonical journal history remain authoritative.

Account names and account codes must never determine accounting behaviour.

==================================================
21. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY
==================================================

Do not unnecessarily prevent:

- future chart customisation;
- additional currencies;
- future markets;
- future VAT/accounting requirements;
- future payment workflows.

Any material policy change must use the Living Product Decisions process.
