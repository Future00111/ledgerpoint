DEC-21 — TENANT ISOLATION AND RLS
STATUS: APPROVED — security/product architecture policy only

Approve the mandatory layered company-isolation policy.

1. FUNDAMENTAL COMPANY BOUNDARY

Every company-owned operation must be explicitly scoped to the relevant company.

This applies to:

- reads;
- writes;
- reports;
- exports;
- background jobs;
- documents;
- AI operations;
- notifications;
- audit operations;
- recovery operations;
- support tooling.

Company scope must be enforced server-side.

2. MEMBERSHIP + CAPABILITY

Every consequential company operation requires both:

- active company membership;
- applicable DEC-05 capability/authority.

Authority in Company A must never grant authority in Company B.

Client-side company selection must never be treated as sufficient authorisation.

3. COMPANY OWNERSHIP

Authoritative company-owned records must have explicit company ownership or an equivalently enforceable company boundary.

Ordinary cross-company references are prohibited.

Do not invent cross-company relationships simply for convenience.

4. ACCOUNTING ISOLATION

Company isolation applies to all accounting concepts, including:

- journals;
- accounts;
- AR/AP;
- VAT;
- financial years;
- accounting periods;
- payments;
- allocations;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- reconciliation;
- configuration.

A transaction belonging to Company A must never affect Company B's accounting.

5. CONFIGURATION ISOLATION

Company-specific configuration remains company-scoped, including:

- financial-year configuration;
- accounting periods;
- chart of accounts;
- control-account mappings;
- configuration versions;
- company settings.

A configuration belonging to one company must never silently influence another company's accounting.

6. DOCUMENT ISOLATION

Documents and source evidence must remain company-scoped, including:

- invoices;
- bills;
- receipts;
- credit notes;
- uploaded documents;
- supporting evidence;
- document downloads;
- document bundles.

Company A must never be able to retrieve Company B's documents through normal product functionality.

7. BANKING AND RECONCILIATION ISOLATION

Company isolation applies to:

- bank evidence;
- bank accounts;
- payments;
- allocations;
- unapplied cash;
- customer credits;
- supplier prepayments;
- refunds;
- reconciliation.

Banking evidence must never become visible to the wrong company.

8. AUDIT / RETENTION / DISPOSAL

Audit and lifecycle records remain company-scoped.

This includes:

- audit history;
- retention information;
- legal holds;
- deletion;
- anonymisation;
- archival;
- redaction;
- disposal events.

A company must never access another company's audit or lifecycle history.

9. EXPORT ISOLATION

DEC-19 exports must always remain company-scoped.

This applies to:

- report exports;
- accounting exports;
- operational exports;
- audit packages;
- document bundles;
- configuration exports.

Filters must never be capable of escaping company scope.

10. BACKUP / RECOVERY ISOLATION

DEC-20 backup and recovery processes must preserve company boundaries.

Recovery must never expose Company A data to Company B.

Where recovery environments are used, company scope must remain enforceable.

11. AI ISOLATION

AI must remain company-scoped.

AI must never use Company A information to answer a Company B request unless a separately approved legitimate cross-company context exists.

This applies to:

- prompts;
- uploaded documents;
- retrieved accounting records;
- context;
- memory;
- generated suggestions;
- AI action history.

AI must not become an accidental cross-company data path.

12. BACKGROUND JOBS

Asynchronous and scheduled processing must preserve originating company scope.

This includes:

- scheduled jobs;
- reconciliation;
- exports;
- AI processing;
- notifications;
- retention jobs;
- disposal jobs;
- other company-specific automation.

Loss of company context in a background process must be treated as a security/integrity failure.

13. SUPPORT / ADMIN ACCESS

Do not create an unrestricted superuser model.

If exceptional cross-company operational access is ever required, it must be:

- explicitly authorised;
- narrowly scoped;
- time-limited;
- reasoned;
- audited;
- subject to appropriate approval.

Break-glass access must not become ordinary product authority.

14. DEFENCE IN DEPTH

Company isolation must not depend on a single UI check or a single filtering mechanism.

Use layered server-side controls across relevant system boundaries.

Database-level RLS is approved as a defence-in-depth mechanism, particularly for high-risk accounting data.

However:

DO NOT assume universal RLS is mandatory for every table or workflow until compatibility with:

- workers;
- connection pooling;
- migrations;
- support operations;
- recovery;
- other relevant infrastructure;

has been validated.

This policy deliberately leaves the exact RLS implementation architecture open.

15. FAILURE HANDLING

Treat any confirmed company-isolation failure as both:

- a security incident;
- an accounting-integrity incident,

where accounting data is affected.

Isolation failures must be detectable, auditable and subject to appropriate incident handling.

16. AUDITABILITY

Maintain appropriate immutable evidence for company-boundary-sensitive events, including:

- company membership changes;
- capability changes;
- privileged access;
- exceptional cross-company access;
- recovery access;
- sensitive export access;
- sensitive document access;
- material isolation/security incidents.

Audit evidence itself must remain appropriately company-scoped and protected.

17. MULTI-COMPANY USERS

A user may belong to multiple companies where supported.

However:

membership in multiple companies does NOT merge their authority.

The active company context must always be explicit for company-scoped operations.

Switching companies must establish a new authorised company context.

18. COMPANY CLOSURE

Company closure does not remove the isolation boundary.

Closed companies remain separate accounting/security domains for as long as their records exist under DEC-17/18.

19. MIGRATION BOUNDARY

DEC-22 remains responsible for migration and cutover policy.

Migration must preserve company boundaries.

It must never:

- merge companies accidentally;
- assign records to the wrong company;
- invent cross-company relationships.

Do not use DEC-21 to resolve DEC-22 migration strategy.

20. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The approved policy does not prohibit future:

- multi-company reporting;
- consolidation;
- group structures;
- intercompany accounting;
- stronger physical isolation;
- regional data residency;
- larger organisational structures.

These are outside launch scope unless separately approved.

21. IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY

This is a policy/architecture approval.

It does NOT authorise implementation of:

- RLS;
- schema changes;
- database changes;
- migrations;
- middleware;
- service-layer changes;
- UI changes;
- infrastructure changes;
- deployment changes.

The exact implementation must be designed and approved through the appropriate implementation process after all required governance decisions are complete.
