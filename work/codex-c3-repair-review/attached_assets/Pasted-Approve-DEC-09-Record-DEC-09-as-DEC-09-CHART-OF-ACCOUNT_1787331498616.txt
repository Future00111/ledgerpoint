DEC-09 — CHART OF ACCOUNTS AND DEFAULT ACCOUNT POLICY
STATUS: APPROVED

Approve the recommended Chart of Accounts policy with the following boundaries:

1. DEFAULT CHART

Ledgerly will provide a versioned UK small-business Chart of Accounts template.

The template is copied into each company rather than remaining a mutable global chart.

The launch chart should be concise and practical, covering the core accounting needs required by the approved launch scope.

2. ACCOUNT TYPES

The authoritative primary account types are:

- Asset
- Liability
- Equity
- Revenue
- Cost of Sales
- Expense

Do not introduce additional primary account types without a future approved decision.

3. ACCOUNT IDENTITIES

Each account must have a stable identity independent of:

- account name;
- account code;
- display label.

Account codes and names are presentation/configuration attributes.

Accounting meaning must be determined through stable identifiers and explicit classifications/mappings.

4. ACCOUNT CODES

Use company-scoped four-digit account codes for readability and practical small-business use.

Codes must not be treated as the authoritative accounting identity.

Changing an account code must not change the accounting meaning of historical postings.

5. DEFAULT CHART CONTENT

The launch template should cover the required core areas, including:

- bank/cash;
- accounts receivable;
- accounts payable;
- VAT;
- equity;
- revenue;
- cost of sales;
- common operating expenses.

Optional chart extensions may exist for areas such as:

- inventory;
- payroll;
- fixed assets;
- corporation tax;
- other future workflows.

The existence of an optional account must NOT imply that the associated workflow is supported at launch.

6. SYSTEM-ACCOUNT PROTECTION

The architecture may identify protected system-account candidates, including:

- Accounts Receivable;
- Accounts Payable;
- Bank/Cash;
- VAT;
- VAT settlement;
- applicable posted retained-results/equity accounts.

However:

DEC-09 does NOT select the final active control-account mappings.

Those mappings remain the responsibility of DEC-10.

Do not resolve DEC-10 as part of this approval.

7. USER CUSTOMISATION

Companies may create and manage custom accounts subject to accounting-integrity controls.

Users may be permitted to:

- create accounts;
- rename accounts where safe;
- change codes where safe;
- maintain permitted metadata;
- deactivate accounts.

However, once an account has been used in accounting or configuration in a way that makes its meaning material:

- its primary account type cannot be changed;
- its reporting classification cannot be changed in a way that changes historical accounting meaning;
- historical postings must retain their original account identity.

8. ACCOUNT DELETION

An account must not be physically deleted once it has any historical or configuration reference.

Use deactivation instead.

Historical journal lines must never lose their account reference because an account becomes inactive.

9. ACCOUNT LIFECYCLE

Use only:

ACTIVE
INACTIVE

for account availability.

Do not introduce a separate HISTORICAL state.

"Historical" is a derived condition based on references.

"SYSTEM" is a protection role/attribute, not a separate account lifecycle state.

10. ACCOUNT MERGING

Do NOT support account merging at launch.

Treat account merging as future scope requiring a separate decision if needed.

Do not rewrite historical journals to simulate a merge.

11. REPORTING

Reporting must derive from:

- stable account identities;
- explicit account classifications;
- canonical journal data.

Reporting must NOT depend on:

- account names;
- account-code ranges;
- fragile frontend logic.

Accounts must support authoritative classification for:

- Profit & Loss;
- Balance Sheet;
- Trial Balance;
- General Ledger;
- relevant VAT reporting.

12. ACCOUNTING INTEGRITY

Chart customisation must never be capable of breaking:

- double-entry accounting;
- immutable posted journals;
- VAT integrity;
- AR;
- AP;
- bank accounting;
- reporting;
- period controls;
- year-end controls.

Account names and codes must never themselves determine accounting behaviour.

13. DEC-03 BOUNDARY

DEC-03 remains the sole VAT authority.

DEC-09 does not create a second VAT engine or expand the approved VAT scope.

14. DEC-04 BOUNDARY

Posted accounting remains immutable.

Changing an account's display name, code, or configuration must never rewrite posted accounting history.

Corrections remain explicit accounting operations.

15. DEC-05 BOUNDARY

Account-management actions must ultimately use the approved server-side capability model.

Do not grant authority merely because a user has a role string or frontend access.

16. DEC-06 / DEC-07 / DEC-08 BOUNDARIES

The chart must operate within:

- DEC-06 financial-year policy;
- DEC-07 monthly accounting-period policy;
- DEC-08 reporting-only year-end policy.

Do not introduce automatic year-end closing journals through DEC-09.

17. MIGRATION

Historical account mapping must be evidence-based.

Never invent historical account mappings.

Where an existing account cannot be confidently mapped, preserve the ambiguity as a migration exception for the later migration decision.

18. DEC-10 SEPARATION

DEC-10 remains responsible for the detailed control-account mapping policy.

Do NOT:

- choose final AR mappings;
- choose final AP mappings;
- choose final VAT control mappings;
- choose invoice/bill/payment posting mappings;
- implement those mappings.

Those are downstream of DEC-10.

19. FUTURE FLEXIBILITY

The approved policy must allow controlled evolution of:

- account names;
- account codes;
- optional accounts;
- reporting classifications;
- future account structures.

Protected system/accounting boundaries remain subject to explicit governance.

Any material future change follows the Living Product Decisions process.
